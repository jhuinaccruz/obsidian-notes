>[!quote] 
>*In Japanese, the passive form (受身形, **うけみけい**) is used to express actions that happen to the subject, often implying that the subject is affected by the action. It is similar to the passive voice in English, but it can also carry nuances of indirectness or politeness. Below is an overview of how passive forms work and are conjugated.*

>[!quote]
>From Genki II:
>*When you are inconvenienced by something somebody else has done, you can express your dissatisfaction using the passive sentence.*
>
>*Passive forms can also be used as a natural passive or when the subject is non-human (e.g. explaining social facts).*

| verb   | passive form |
| ------ | ------------ |
| う-verb | ~られる         |
| る-verb | -あれる         |
| 来る     | こられる         |
| する     | される
