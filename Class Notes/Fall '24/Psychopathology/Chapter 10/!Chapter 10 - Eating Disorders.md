In the [[DSM-5]], there are two main types of eating disorders:
- [[Bulimia Nervosa]]
- [[Anorexia Nervosa]]
- [[Binge Eating Disorder]]

## Binge Eating
>Bingeing is the hallmark of bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder

Bingeing: eating excess amounts of food in a discrete period of time
	Eating is perceived as uncontrollable and can lead to feelings of guilt, shame, or regret

Binge-eating can also result in hiding behavior(s) from family members

>Consumed foods are often high in sugar, fat, or carbohydrates

## Bulimia Nervosa
>Binges that are accompanied by compensatory behaviors

Compensatory measures include:
- purging
- excessive exercise
- fasting or food restriction

Most people with bulimia nervosa are within 10% of normal body weight

Most people with bulimia nervosa are overconcerned with body shape, with fear of gaining weight
	additionally, most have comorbid psychological disorders

## Anorexia Nervosa
>Extreme weight loss is the hallmark of anorexia

The restriction of calories results in low energy

An intense fear of weight gain results in body-image distortion

### Binge-Eating Disorder
>Characterized by binge eating without associated compensatory behaviors

Binge-eating disorder is associated instead with distress and/or functional impairment
	Concern with weight or shape does not have to be present

>Approximately 20% of individuals in weight-control programs have BED

>Approximately half of candidates for bariatric surgery have BED

People with BED generally respond better to treatment than other eating disorders

## Treatment Options
### Bulimia Nervosa
- __[[Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy]]__: can be used to address distorted body image and maladaptive attempts to control weight
- __[[Antidepressants]]__: can address bingeing/purging cycles

### Binge-Eating Disorder
>[!warning]
>Once-used obesity medications are no longer recommended as a viable treatment for BED

- __Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy__ is considered effective
- __Interpersonal Psychotherapy__: equally as effective as CBT
- __Self-help__ Techniques such as Overeaters Anonymous are considered effective

### Anorexia Nervosa
>[!important]
>All treatments begin with attaining a healthy weight first

- __Psychoeducation__ like behavioral and cognitive interventions relating to food, weight, body image, thoughts, and emotions
- __Family focus__ has shown the most support in clinical trials relating to the treatment of adolescents with anorexia
