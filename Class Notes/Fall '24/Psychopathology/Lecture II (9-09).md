## Historical Conceptions of Abnormal Behavior

By and large, there have been three dominant "traditions"

- ### Supernatural
	defined as a battle between "good" and "evil" external forces (ex. spirits, powers, deities, etc.)
	
	treatments included exorcisms, torture, and rituals
	
	also in that same tradition, "insanity" was not related to supernatural forces, but rather "emotional stress"
		treated with rest, sleep, healthy environment, and/or potions

- ### Biological
	defined illness and disorders as a direct consequence of physical symptoms
	
	==Hippocrates== (460-377 BC)
	- considered father of modern medicine
	- linked "abnormality" with brain chemical imbalances
		Hippocrates called them the four "humeurs"
	- foreshadowed modern medical opinions
	- his ideas were later extended by Galen (129-198 AD)
	- coined hysteria: the "wandering uterus"
		a woman's emotion a direct result from the uterus "moving around" in the body

	general paresis: mental illness induced by syphilis, lead to an understanding that the body is interconnected

	Phineas Gage
	- suffered an accident, had a metal rod shoved from cheek to the frontal lobe
		consequently, his behavior and personality changed, became a celebrity as a medical mystery 

	Reforms of psychiatric care
	- Insulin Shock Therapy
		initially very drastic and often grotesque
	- Electroconvulsive Therapy
		still in use today, much more regulated since its inception
	- Psychosurgery

- ### Psychological
	defined illnesses and disorders using social contexts and psychological development

	Moral therapy
	- Established by Dorothea Dix (1802 - 1887)
