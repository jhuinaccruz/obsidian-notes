>According to Gemini:
>
>*In statistics, "shape" refers to the shape of a data distribution, which visually describes how data points are spread out across the range of possible values. Imagine plotting your data points on a graph; the resulting pattern reveals the distribution's shape.*
>
>*Here's why understanding shape is important:*
>- *__Reveals patterns__: The shape provides insights into the underlying data patterns. For example, a symmetrical bell curve indicates a normal distribution, while a long tail to the right suggests a skewed distribution.*
>- *__Guides analysis__: Different statistical tests and analyses are appropriate for different distribution shapes. Knowing the shape helps you choose the right tools.*
>- *__Aids interpretation__: The shape helps you understand the data's story. For instance, a bimodal distribution (two peaks) might suggest two distinct groups within your data.*

Shape can be measured in a number of different ways, including:
- __Skewness__ (also known as __Symmetry__)
	- Negative: to the left
	- Positive: to the right
- __Kurtosis__ (also known as flatness)
	measures the peakness and tailedness of a distribution
	>Leptokurtic: kurtosis > 3
	>Platykurtic: kurtosis < 3
	>Mesokurtic: kurtosis = 3
- __Excess Kurtosis__: adjusts kurtosis such that normal distribution has a distribution of 0
