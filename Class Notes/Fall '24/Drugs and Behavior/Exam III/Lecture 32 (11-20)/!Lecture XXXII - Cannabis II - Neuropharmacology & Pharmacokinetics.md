## Synthocannabinoid
Also known as "cannabinoid-like chemicals"
- Most are produced in China
- Were once unregulated and can contain many other drugs (including [[Hallucinogens]] and [[Methamphetamine]]-like compounds as well as products like rat poison and embalming fluid)
- Can produce extreme anxiety, confusion, and even psychotic effects
- Can be far more potent than THC (40-100x more potent)

## Endocannabinoids
Endocannabinoids are cannabinoid receptor agonists synthesized by the body

Two main endocannabinoids:
- __Arachidonoyl ethanolamide__ (anandamide or AEA)
	first discovered in 1994 (as "internal bliss")
- __2-arachidonoyglycerol__ (2-AG)

These are __retrograde messengers__ (take messages from *postsynapse* to the *presynapse*)

>Endocannabinoids are fatty acids synthesized from phospholipids in the cell membrane and released in response to synaaptic activity

They are released by the post-synaptic cell to activate presynaptic CB1 receptors
- produce complex effects through the body
- considered a [[Neuromodulator]]

## Cannabinoid Receptors
First identified in 1988

Two main receptors
- CB1 receptors are primarily found in the brain and [[Central Nervous System]]
	- They are metabotropic and inhibitory
- CB2 receptors are mostly found in in the peripheral organs (especially cells associated with the immune system)

### Retrograde Signaling
1. Neurotransmitter release from the presynaptic neuron triggers postsynaptic activity
2. Stimulated postsynaptic neuron synthesized and released endocannabinoids (such as anandemide) as needed
3. Endocannabinoids disseminate back and bind to the presynaptic CB1 receptor
4. CB1 receptor stimulates a G-protein, leading to the inhibition of presynaptic neurotransmitter release
5. Phyto- and synthocannabinoids are thought to activate CB1 receptors directly, mimicking the effects of endocannabinoids on presynaptic neurons

### Dopamine Release in the nAc 
- Stimulation of CB1 receptors on GABA terminals *reduces* the amount of GABA released onto dopamine projection neurons
	- This disinhibits dopamine neurons, resulting in increased dopamine release in the [[Nucleus Accumbens]] (important for reinforcing effects of THC)

## Pharmacokinetics
### Absorption
>Cannabinoids are weak acids and highly lipid soluble

Can be
- inhaled
	- peaks in the blood in 6-10 minutes
	- peak behavioral effects: 30-60 minutes
- orally administered
	- peak effects: 1-3 hours

>Blood THC levels decline rapidly after smoking cannabis, but complete elimination from the body is much slower because of persistence in fat tissues (>2 weeks)
