### Distribution
Bioavailability is 10-35% and 4-12% via ingestion

Half-life (in blood) is 1-3 days from occasional use or 5-12 days for chronic users

[[THC]] can remain in body fat for many weeks

### Metabolism
>Metabolism starts as soon as the cannabinoids enter the body

Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabidiol is converted to 11-hydroxy-delta-9-tetrahydrocannabidiol
	This form is more active than THC and crosses the [[Blood Brain Barrier]] more easily

## Effects
### Physiological
- cardiac acceleration
- dry mouth
- reddening of the eyes
- drooping of the eyelids (due to muscle relaxation)
- drowsiness and increased sleep time
	higher doses can interfere with sleep
- often causes hyperphagia due to interactions with appetite control
	"NPY" neurons in the hypothalamus
- Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome (CHS): recurrent nausea, vomiting, and cramping abdominal pain that can occur due to prolonged, high-dose cannabis use
	This mechanism is poorly understood

> Approximately six million near-daily marijuana users in the U.S. have symptoms of CHS, which can be life-threatening if left untreated

### Psychological/Subjective
- euphoria
- relaxation
- giddiness
- elevated mood ("contagious laughter")
- subjective perceptual changes including:
	- distorted perception of distance and time (usually elongated)
	- intensification of colors
	- blending of patterns and objects
- cognitive impairments in attention and memory that interfere with complex visuomotor skills

>Sensory alternations are probably due to THC's effect on CB1 receptors in areas important for sensory processing, including sensory cortices, the inferior colliculus and posterior thalamus

>Temporal disintegration (time distortions) likely involve the cerebellum, since it's known to fine tune rhythmic activity, allowing us to estimate time

>Cannabis users are 5x as likely to develop psychotic disorders

>Some studies suggest that cannabis use impairs intelligence

>A four decades long longitudinal study of more than 1000 people from birth to age 38 showed that participants who reported heavy/daily cannabis use early in life experienced on average an eight-point drop in IQ score between childhood and adulthood compared to those who were not regular cannabis users (Meier et al., 2012)

>Teens with daily marijuana use had an abnormally-shaped hippocampus and performed 18% more poorly on long-term memory tasks

### Memory

>No effect on the ability to recall material already well learned or on recognition memory

>Does disrupt the ability to recall words or narrative material

Cannabis can disrupt short-term memory functions

Consumption can also lead to temporal disintegration, or the loss of ability to "keep things in mind"

>Activation of hippocampal CB1 receptors are responsible for THC-induced memory impairments

## Experiments

>Animals will self-administer THC and other cannabinoids

>Humans and squirrel monkeys will self-administer THC but self-administration studies with rodents has yielded inconsistent results

The effect on rodents is most likely due to a history of a delayed onset or solubility/absorption issues, and likely does not reflect a lack of the reinforcing properties of THC
	This is supported by evidence around rodents forming a conditioned placed preference for THC

## [[DSM-5]]

[[Cannabis Use Disorder]]: Frequent use can lead to the diagnosis of Cannabis Uee Disorder

Risk of developing CUD is higher in recreational users who:
1. initiated use during adolescence
2. smoke high potency cannabis on a daily basis

>About 9% of people who use cannabis may become dependent; 1 in 6 who start use in adolescence

## Tolerance
Many people that use cannabis report _reverse tolerance_ from repeated and continued use

Tolerance to cardiovascular and behavioral responses can also occur

## Withdrawal
>Chronic cannabis use may result in physical and psychological dependance

Relatively minor, can however increase proportional to the amount of THC consumed
