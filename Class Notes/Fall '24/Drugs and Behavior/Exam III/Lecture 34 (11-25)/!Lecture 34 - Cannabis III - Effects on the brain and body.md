## Effects

Intellectual/Cognitive Impairment
>Some studies suggest that cannabis use (especially when begun early in life) impairs intelligence, while others do not

>A four decades long longitudinal study of more than 1000 people from birth to age 38 showed that participants who reported heavy/daily cannabis use early in life experienced on average an eight point drop in IQ score between childhood and adulthood compared to those who were not regular cannabis users (Meier et al., 2012)

# Hallucinogens
>Hallucinogenic drugs alter thought, perception, and mood

__[[Hallucinogens]]__ are defined as "agents" that can alter thought, perception and mood without producing memory or intellectual impairment, delirium, or addiction
	Hallucinogens are also defined by their minimal autonomic side effects

## Classic Hallucinogens
__[[Classic Hallucinogens]]__ like [[LSD]], [[Psilocybin]], and [[Mescaline]] can produce "otherworldly" sensory experiences
	Reported experiences can also include a perceived spiritual or psychological meaning
>Users of classic hallucinogens oen report gaining personal insight or empathy, and may feel more in touch with other people, one’s own self, God, or the universe, and may experience a state of ecstasy

Classic hallucinogens include
- __[[Lysergic acid amide]]__ (LSA): found in the seeds of the [[Morning Glory Plant]] and [[Ergot fungus]] that grows on rye/wheat grain
- __Lysergic acid diethylamide__ ([[LSD]]): Usually synthesized, but similar forms are found in Ergot fungus
- __[[Psilocybin]]__: found in "shrooms" (genus [[Psilocybe]])
- __[[Bufotenine]]__: found in skin secretions of some toads and certain plants
- __[[Harmine]]/Harmaline__: found in tropical vines of South America
- __N,N-Dimethyltryptamine__ ([[DMT]]): found in many South American plants (like ayahuasca) and in some insect and amphibian species
- __[[Mescaline]]__: found in the peyote cactus native to deserts of Mexico and the southwestern US

Other names for hallucinogenic and related drugs:
- Psychedelics: drugs that change one's level of consciousness
- Phantasticants: drugs that induce vivid perceptual experiences
- Empathogens: drugs that induce feelings of empathy
- Entactogens: induces "touching within" oneself
- Psychotomimetics: drugs that can induce psychosis and delirium
- Club Drugs: recreational drugs used at dance clubs and raves
## Visual Hallucinations
### Stage I:
>In 1845 Jacques-Joseph Moreau, a young French psychiatrist, took hashish and self-analyzed his hallucinatory state
>Conclusion: hallucinations result from brain "excitation"

Kluver in 1966 established 4 constant geometric shapes in a mescaline-induced imagery:
1. tunnel/funnel/alley
2. spirals
3. grating/lattice/honeycomb
4. cobwebs
>He discovered that these constants appeared in other hallucinatory conditions as well

### Stage II
>Other researchers have described a second stage, involving animals, people (often caricatures) and places, seen by ~60-70% of subjects

## Studies

R.K. Siegel (1977) - what are some common themes of hallucinations?:
- are similar across different cultures
- drug and non-drug hallucinations are similar
- concluded that nature and structure of hallucinations are determined by brain activity rather than the drug itself

## Models
### Neurological Model of Visual Hallucinations

Proposed by Bressloff et al:
>"Hallucinatory images in the brain could be understood in terms of spontaneous pattern formation in the brain... primary visual cortex makes pattern of activity when it goes *unstable*."
