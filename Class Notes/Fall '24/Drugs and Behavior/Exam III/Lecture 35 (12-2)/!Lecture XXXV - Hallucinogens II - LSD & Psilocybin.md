#### Lysergic Acid Amide/Lysergic Acid Diethylamide

>Related amides are found in ergot, a toxic rye/grain fungus that has been responsible for thousands of deaths over the years

__Ergotism__: poisoning produced by ergot, whihc results in a headache, vomiting, diarrhea and gangrene of the fingers and toes
	Tied to a popular theory that explains the causes of the witch trials in Salem (Linnda Caporael hypothesized that the Salem witch trials followed a rye ergot outbreak)

## Neuropharmacology
### Psilocybin/Psilocin & LSD/LSA
>Psilocybin itself has low psychoactive properties (it’s a “prodrug”), but is metabolized into psilocin in the body, which, along with LSD, binds to 5-HT2A receptors as a partial agonist (generates a weaker response than serotonin) in cortex and subcortical structures.

Psilocybin can also interact with other 5-HTR subtypes as well as [[catecholamine]] receptors (such as [[dopamine]]) and the 5-HT transporter

>LSA, LSD, and psilocin are selective partial agonists of the metabotropic [[serotonin]] 2A receptor

On a larger scale, these drugs can increase glutamate transmission in the prefrontal cortex (known as "cortical overload")

The excitation of the [[Prefrontal Cortex]]'s glutamate neurons is thought to disrupt the gating of sensory and cognitive input from subcortical areas (such as the thalamus, resulting in the perceptual and cognitive distortions of a "trip")
	When these neurons are activated, they amplify sensory perception, cognition, and mood due to the more sensory information reaching conscious awareness, leading to vivid and intense hallucinations

>Brain imaging has also shown less integrated "default mode network" cortical activity, explaining changes in states of consciousness (such as the production of "non-ordinary states of consciousness" induced by these drugs)
