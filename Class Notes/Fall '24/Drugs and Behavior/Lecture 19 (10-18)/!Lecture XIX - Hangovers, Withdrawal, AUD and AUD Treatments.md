## [[Alcohol Use Disorder]]

### [[DSM-5]]
It contains 11 criteria, including tolerance and withdrawal characteristics

AUD can be graded as *mild* (2-3 criteria met), *moderate* (4-5 criteria met), or *severe* (6 or more criteria met)

### Chronic Effects of Alcohol
#### Dependence
Can manifest itself in two forms
- __[[Early Minor Syndrome]]__
	about eight hours post drinking, where symptoms include
	- weakness and tremors
	- anxiety, agitation, and irritability
	- restlessness and trouble sleeping
	- nausea and vomiting
	- sweating
	- elevated blood pressure, pulse, and respiratory tone
	- irregular heartbeat
- __[[Late Major Syndrome]]__ ("delirium tremens")
	may occur when abstinent for a continued period of time (2-3 days of abstinence)
	- can last as long as 7-10 days
	- is associated with an 8-10% mortality rate without any medical assistence
	- symptoms include:
		- irregular heart rate
		- high body temperature
		- agitation
		- aggression
		- irritability
		- disorientation/confusion
		- delusions or paranoid thoughts
		- hallucinations
		- hyperexcitability leading to tremors and/or seizures
### Treatments
- __12-Step Program__ (Alcoholics Anonymous)
	- requires relying on a higher power to control drinking
- __Cognitive_Behavioral Therapy__
	- relapse prevention, self-control and coping-skills training
- __Pharmacotherapy__ (Drug Treatments)
	- [[Naltrexone]]
		makes drinking less pleasurable by blocking opioid receptors to reduce alcohol's rewarding effects and cravings
	- [[Acamprosate]]
		inhibits the release of glutamate, thus decreasing hyperexcitation that occurs during withdrawal (making it less severe)
	- [[Antabuse]]
		blocks aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH)
# Anxiolytics & Sedative-Hypnotics
## Symptoms
__Physical__ symptoms include:
- sweating
- tachycardia
- upset stomach
- shortness of breath
- frequent urination or diarrhea
- sleep disturbances
- fatigue
__Psychological/emotional__ symptoms include:
- excessive fear and worry
- irritability
- restlessness
- trouble concentrating
- feeling tense

## History

>Alcohol and opium were physicians’ drugs of choice as calming/sleep agents until the late 19th century, when modern anxiolytics were developed

- __Mid 1800s__: __[[Ether]]__ and __[[Chloroform]]__ were introduced and used widely in the United States Civil War
	later they were found to be dangerous (can induce cardiac arrest)
- __Late 1800s__: __[[Barbiturate]]s__ were invented and became the standard for almost a century, became illegal later in the __1960s__ as "downers"
- __1950s__: __[[Methaqualone]]__ (quaaludes) were synthesized in India as a "safer" alternative to barbiturates, but misuse in the United States led them to become a [[Schedule I]] drug
	also not very safe

>The perceived public need for anti-anxiety (vs. primarily sedative-hypnotic) effects along with safer alternatives, spurred research for new drugs

- __1950s - now__: __[[Benzodiazepine]]s__ were synthesized with a better [[Therapeutic Index]] than barbiturates and less addictive potential
	ex. [[Valium]], [[Xanax]], and [[Rophynol]] (date rape drug)

### Anxiolytics and Sedative-Hypnotics
>Anxiolytics (fka “tranquilizers”) treat agitation and anxiety disorders

>Sedative-Hypnotics are drugs that are used to sedate (anesthetic), and/or aid in sleep (i.e. “sleeping pills”)

Many drugs are considered both anxiolytics and sedative-hypnotics, such as
- __Barbiturates__ like [[amobarbital]] and [[secobarbital]]
- __Methaqualone__ like [[Quaalude]]
- __Benzodiazepines__ like [[alprazolam]] and [[diazepam]]
- __[[Z-drug]]s__ like [[Ambien]] and [[Lunesta]]

>With increasing doses, the level of awareness and arousal gradually decreases along a continuum until death occurs
