## Neuropharmacology of Nicotine

>Nicotine increases dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) in a dose-dependent manner

## Peripheral Autonomic Effects of Nicotine
### Sympathomimetic
increases (partly due to epinepherine):
- heart rate
- blood preasure
- respiration
- skin vasoconstriction ()

### Parasympathomimetic
- increased smooth muscle activity
	increased stomach and bowel activity (laxative properties)

## Central Effects of Nicotine
- Nausea
- Arousal (midbrain areas)
- Improvements in aspects of motor and cognitive performance like attention, short term episodic memory and fine motor skills

### Subjective Effects of Nicotine
- Stimulation and mood enhancement/pleasure
- Increases in dopamine release in the nAc

### Nicotine Bolus Theory of Addiction:
>The sudden delivery of concentrated nicotine intensifies pleasure and enhances reinforcing effects, making nicotine from smoking/vaping far more addicting than when administered via slower routes

## Nicotine Dependence/Withdrawal

### Physical Dependence
- most symptoms develop after several days and peak after a week
- most are gone after one month (excluding cravings)

### Psychological Dependence
unlike most other drugs, there is a strong craving ot return to tobacco use 

## Harm and Risk of Nicotine Consumption
- increased risk of cardiovascular diseases
- increased risk of cancers
- increased risk of respiratory diseases

# Psychomotor Stimulants 1: Sources

## Psychomotor Stimulants (Psychostimulants)
- Drugs that enhance wakefulness and alertness, reduce fatigue, and activate the motor system
### Sympathomimetic
- increased heart rate and blood pressure
- bronchodilation

### Naturally-Occuring
- Ephedrine
	has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for more than 5000 years
	- very similar to epinephrine
	- is stable and has a longer form unlike epinepherine
	- originally used to treat asthma
- Cathinone (khat/catha edulis)
	- a perennial shrub found in east Africa
	- containes cathinone, a psychostimulant structurally similar to amphetamine
	- released by chewing the fresh leaves or pulverizing them into a powder and mixing with tea
	- it's estimated that 10-20 million people chew Khat regularly
	- unstable and degrades rapidly
- Cocaine (Erythroxylum coca)
	- extracted from the coca bush
