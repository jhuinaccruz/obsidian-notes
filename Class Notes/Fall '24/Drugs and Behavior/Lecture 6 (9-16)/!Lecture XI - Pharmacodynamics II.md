## [[Routes of Absorbing Substances]]

__Bioavailability__: how much of a substance becomes available to its intended biological destination
 
Routes of absorption:

### Inhalation
- quick, efficient gas exchange
### Membrane
- includes intranasal (fastest), sublingual, and transdermal (least fast)
### Oral
- involves the digestive system
	although sublingual is transdermal, it doesn't go through the digestive system
- in order to be absorbed into the bloodstream, the drug must pass through the digestive system and the capillary wall
	leads to slower and lower bioavailability

## Metabolization
__First-pass Metabolism__: the process of a substance consumed orally being absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract

### Distribution
Drugs are spread everywhere in the body (through the blood) but its distribution can be affected by several factors including:
- Lipid solubility (drugs can sequester in fat)
- Active and Passive Transport Across Membranes
- Protein Binding
- Placental Barrier (for pregnant people)
- Distribution to the Central Nervous System (Blood Brain Barrier)
