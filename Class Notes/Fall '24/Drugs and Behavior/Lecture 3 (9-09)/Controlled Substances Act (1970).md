> *The Controlled Substances Act (CSA) of 1970 is a federal U.S. drug policy that regulates the manufacture, importation, possession, use, and distribution of certain narcotics, stimulants, depressants, hallucinogens, anabolic steroids, and other chemicals. It places these substances into one of five schedules based on their medical use, potential for abuse, and safety or dependence liability.*

- Schedule I - high potential for misuse, no acceptable medical use
>[!example] heroine, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD)
- Schedule II - can lead to sever psychological or physical dependance
>[!example] morphine, methamphetamine. cocaine, methadone
- Schedule III - can lead to moderate or low physical dependance, or high psychological dependance
>[!example] anabolic steroids, codeine, hydrocodone
- Schedule IV - can lead to limited physical dependance or psychological dependance
>[!example] xanax, calium, ativan
- Schedule V - drugs with low potential for abuse
>[!example] cough medicine with less than 200mg of codeine per 100 mL
