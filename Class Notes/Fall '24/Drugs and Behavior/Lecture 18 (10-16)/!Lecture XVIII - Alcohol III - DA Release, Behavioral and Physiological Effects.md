## Pharmacodynamics (contd.)
>Reward craving (positive reinforcement) may result from alcohol's indirect impact on dopaminergic and opiodergic systems

### Effects on Dopaminergic Systems
>Alcohol increases [[Dopamine]] release in the striatum

More specifically, the [[Nucleus Accumbens]]
	This can only be part of alcohol's reinforcing effects, due to evidence that effective treatment for [[Alcohol Use Disorder]] acts on the opioid system

>Both the euphorid and reinforcing effects of ethanol are blocked by naltrexone (opioid receptor antagonist)

>The VTA contains iinhibtory GABAergic interneurons, glutamatergic neurons, and dopamininergic projection neurons that terminate in the ventral striatum and receives opiodergic inputs

#### Dopamine Release
[[Ventral Striatum]] dopamine neurons project to the [[Nucleus Accumbens]]
	these are under continuous inhibition by [[GABA]]ergic interneurons

Said GABAergic neurons express presynaptic [[Mu-Opioid Receptors]] (MORs)
	These turn off the GABA neurons when activated by [[endorphins]]

__Alcohol__ activates opiodergic neurons to release endorphins, which disinhibits VTA dopamine neurons by turning the GABA neurons off

>This process results in *increased release of dopamine* in the nucleus accumbens

## Effects on Behavior
>More modern terms (of being drunk) suggest some sort of violence or damage

Effects on behavior are Blood Alcohol Dependent...:
- as BAC increases, so can its stimulant effects
- as BAC decreases, its sedative effects increase

... as well as biphasic:
- in high-sensory input environments, lower doses appear stimulatory
- in quieter environments, as BAC falls, CNS depression is more pronounced

Hangover symptoms peak once BAC reaches zero

Hangovers occur due to the toxic effects of acetaldehyde including
- inflammation
- disrupted sleep
- vasodilation/headache
- Gi tract disturbance
- low blood sugar

## Acute Physiological Effects

At moderate levels:
- loss of body heat (due to blood vessel dilation)
- increased excretion of water
- increase in heart rate
- disturbed patterns of sleep

At high levels:
- life-threatening consequences
- inhibition of respiratory systems in the medulla at a BAC of about 0.5
- therapeutic index is only about 3.3
- adverse effects of high doses include:
	- lack of memory attention, encoding, storage and retrieval
	- fragmentary blackouts (grayout) and en bloc blackouts 
- impairment in sensorimotor skills
- increased potential for aggressive or violent acts (due to disinhibition of aggression circuits)
