## Dopamine

>Since the 1950s, neuroscientists have understood that dopamine plays a role in how the brain processes rewarding experiences

Before it was thought to be the "master molecule of addiction"

>Roy Wise (1980 article): “Dopamine is involved in creating experiences of pleasure, euphoria, or yumminess”

### Dopamine/Hedonia Hypothesis

Coined by Olds and Milner in the 1950s
	called the mesolimbic pathway the "pleasure center of the brain"
>Rats would readily lever press for microsimulation of this pathway (reinforcing), causing dopamine release, and people with implanted electrodes reported experiencing intense pleasure when this area was stimulated

__Hedonia Hypothesis__: Dopamine release in the mesolimbic pathway *causes* pleasure with in turn *reinforces* behavior(s)
#### Opposing Evidence

>People with Parkinson’s disease (who have a dopamine deficiency) still report experiencing pleasure, and increasing dopamine levels do not increase feelings of pleasure or “liking” (self-reporting)

>Blocking dopamine receptors with pimozide (dopamine receptor antagonist) did not reduce feelings of amphetamine-induced euphoria/pleasure in humans (self-reporting)

>Destroying dopamine components of the mesolimbic pathway do not reduce “liking” reactions in rodents in response to sucrose administration
>Antagonizing dopamine receptors in this pathway do not decrease “liking” reactions

Instead of a single "pleasure center"...__Hedonistic Hotspots__, such as the nucleus accumbens and the adjacent ventral pallidum
- also includes parabrachial nucleus and the pons

>Increases in “liking” reactions result from stimulation of these hotspots and damage to these regions reduce “liking” reactions and in some cases even result in “disliking” reactions to sweet tastes (“anhedonia”)

However, it is independent of dopamine signaling

Said Hedonic Hotspots can be potentiated by [[Endocannabinoid]]s, [[GABA]], and especially [[Opioid]]s

### Changes in Understanding

>Roy Wise, in the late 1990s, asserted that he no longer believed “that the amount of pleasure felt is proportional to the amount of dopamine floating around in the brain”

### Incentive-Salience Hypothesis

Experiments blocking dopamine receptors or destroying axon terminals in the nucleus accumbens resulted in reduced *motivation* to pursue hedonistic activities

Patients with Parkinson's are at an increased risk to show compulsive behaviors (such as gambling)

In another experiment, [[fMRI]] scans show that there is increased dopamine activity in the medial prefrontal cortex, nucleus accumbens and the ventral tegmental area when engaging in behaviors such as gambling. Repeated dopamine signaling in these areas results in neuroplasticity important for reinforcing and motivating said behavior(s)

>[!important]
>All psychoactive drugs *increase* dopamine activity in the brain

### Incentive-Sensitization Theory

When addiction develops, dopamine increases the salience of drugs and stimuli associated with drug consumption
	Perhaps contrarily, the "liking" of a drug decreases due to [[pharmacodynamic]] tolerance

>Neutral cues associated with drug use also gain incentive salience, having been associated with the drug’s euphoric effects based on past experiences
