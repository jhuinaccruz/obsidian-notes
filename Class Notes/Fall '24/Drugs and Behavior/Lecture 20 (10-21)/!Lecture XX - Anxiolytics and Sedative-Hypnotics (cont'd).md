## Neuropharmacology of Barbituates and Benzodiazepines

>Like [[alcohol]], BDZs ([[benzodiazepine]]s), [[barbiturate]]s, and “Z” drugs can be generally classified as GABA-A receptor agonists

Benzodiazepines and Z drugs have their own unique binding site on the alpha-1 subunit
	they *enhance* the effects of GABA, as they enhance chloride ion conductance and increase the affinity with which GABA binds to the binding site

Barbiturates can do what benzodiazepines do at a different site, but they can open chloride channels without GABA signaling, thereby making them less safe

### Addictive Properties
>BDZs, barbiturates and “Z” drugs have all been shown to increase DA release in the NAc (above)

Barbiturates are the most reinforcing in infusion experiments
	results in *wanting/craving*

#### Addiction Mechanisms

Benzodiazepines increase dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens by disenhibiting dopamine neurons in the ventral tegemental area
	This effect is achieved through the inhibition of local GABA interneurons

>Many benzodiazepines activate the alpha-1, but not the alpha-3 subunits

>More than 80% of VTA interneurons have GABA receptors with alpha-1 subunits

## History of Tobacco

Tobacco was first cultivated in the Andes and later spread to North America
> may have first been used as pest control by fumigating crops with tobacco smoke

in 1492 Columbus was given the leaf as a gift by indigenous El Salvadorians, throwing it away as "a thing much appreciated among them" without understanding its use
	Rodrigo de Jerez took up smoking, taking it back to Europe where he was imprisoned for smoking as a "devilish habit"

John Rolfe increased the production in his Virginia colony from 20,000 in 1618 to 500,000 in 1627 to 1,500,000 in 1629

Europeans later smoked it in a pipe and as snuff (dry pulverized leafs)

In the 1800s, tobacco chewing and later cigar smoking became popular

in the 1900s, cigarettes became popular across males in the United States, starting with Camel in 1913 and throughout World War I
	from 1925 to 1935 Lucky Strikes became increasingly popular with women

The Cigarette Explosion resulted around World War II, where American soldiers had cigarettes included in a soldier's rations

Later in the 1950s, scientific research was surfacing linking smoking to lung cancer
	The tobacco industry silenced such research well into the 90s with "safer" products (e.g. lower tar content and filtered cigarettes)

### Tobacco Legislation

In 1964, the Surgeon General made its first statement on the connection between smoking and adverse health consequences
