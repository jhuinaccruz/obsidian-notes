>*Extinction trial spacing across days differentially impacts adult and adolescent male mice*

## Introduction
>*Fear and the ability to control it changes as a function of age - this may be linked to risk for anxiety in youths*

>*Extinction is a tried-and-true way to examine fear regulation in rodents, but little has been done during adolescence. What little has been done suggest standard extinction protocols are less effective for adolescents*

>*Other studies have shown that modifying the protocol through which extinction is learned can benefit adolescents*

## Research Question
>*How does trial spacing across days impact extinction during adolescence*

## Hypothesis
>*Adolescents, like adults, will benefit from spacing trials rather than massing trials*

## Methods

__Subjects__: Adult (PND* 64-83, n = 97) and adolescent (PND 29-31, n = 89) mice (C57bl/6N)

### Procedures
- Pavlovian fear conditioning (tone CS -> shock US)
- Fear extinction (40 tone CS - no US; one day, two days, or four days of training)
- Fear recall test (11 tone CS - no US; 24 hours, 2 weeks, 5 weeks after extinction)

Different kinds of extinction included:
- Massed
- Spaced
- Hybrid
- None

__Dependent Variable__: Freezing

## Results
### Adults
>*During extinction, using either the spaced or hybrid approach worked best to reduce fear in adults*

>*Spaced and hybrid approaches seemed to work best at a short-term test*

>*Opportunities to rehearse and consolidate are beneficial for translating learning into memory*

>*When additional time passed, all approaches left fear behavior susceptible to spontaneous recovery*
