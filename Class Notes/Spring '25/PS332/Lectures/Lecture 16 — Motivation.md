>*Behavior is motivated*

__Behavior is motivated because__:
- Behavior is variable: It can change even given the same history of reinforcement or under the same stimulus conditions, where different levels of motivation can help explain variability in behavior
- Behavior is persistent: It can keep going despite periods of non-reinforcement

## Behavior Motivation
### Goal-Oriented
>*Animals do not just learn rigid associations between a stimulus and a response; Behavior is inherently flexible and a means to an end*

### Reinforcers
>*Reinforcement is not necessary for learning, although it is necessary to motivate the animal to translate what it has learned into performance*

>*Learning and performance are separable constructs*

### Incentive Learning
>*For a motivational state to energize a behavior, the animal must first learn that the action leads to a particular reinforcer, and that this reinforcer has a positive effect on the motivational state*

>*Hunger does not blindly energize all behaviors, rather it selects a specific set of behaviors that will lead to the goal of ending hunger*

>*The effect of a motivational state on behavior depends on what has been learned about the reinforcer while in the motivational state*

### Anticipation
>*A great deal of motivated behavior seems to anticipate, rather than respond to, need*

>*An evolutionarily well adapted animal will not wait until it is in a state of need to begin seeking reinforcers, like food or water. This helps prevent trouble if resources are unexpectedly scarce*

>*Animals must perform behaviors to respond to depletion — but these should be saved for emergencies rather than the everyday state of affairs*

## Paradoxical Reward Effects
### Contrast
__Contrast__: The reward on performance is partly determined by past history of reinforcement
- Positive: Experience with a smaller reward makes a larger reward feel especially large
- Negative: Experience with a larger reward makes a smaller reward feel especially small

__Frustration__: Occurs when an animal experiences negative contrast (expecting a large reward and getting a smaller or no reward). It is an umpleasant emotion, evoking withdrawal behaviors

__Frustration is proportional to__: The difference between the expected and received reward

>*Frustration is also conditioned through classical conditioning. A frustration reaction can be elicited by cues associated with decreases in reward*

### Intrinsic and Extrinsic
__Punishment by reward__: When rewarding performance decreases motivation for non-rewarded performance

## The Partial Reinforcement Extinction Effect

__Partial Reinforcement Extinction Effect__ (PREE): Partially reinforced behavior is more persistent, or resistant to extinction, than continuously reinforced behavior

>PREE tells that there is more to learning than associative strength

>*PREE provides a way to help make behavior more persistent
