## LIterature Research
1. __Define Your Research Question__:
	*Start by clearly defining your research question or topic. This will help you stay focused during your literature search*
2. __Select the Right Database__:
	*Choose appropriate academic databases (e.g. PubMed, Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, JSTOR) relevant to your field of study*
3. __Use Keywords and Boolean Operators__
	*Brainstorm keywords and phrases related to your topic. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to combine and refine your search terms*
4. __Keep Track of Your Searches__
	*Record your search queries, including the keywords and filters you used. This can help you replicate your search if needed*
5. __Utilize Filters and Advanced Search Options__
	*Most databases offer filters and advanced search options to narrow down to specify publication dates, document types, or other relevant criteria*
6. __Read Abstracts and Titles First__
	*When reviewing search results, focus on abstracts and titles initially to quickly assess whether an article is relevant to your research*
7. __Expand and Narrow Your Source__
	*Be flexible with your search terms. If you're not finding enough relevant material, try broader keywords. If you're overwhelmed with results, make your search more specific*
8. __Consider the Source__
	*Evaluate the credibility and reputation of the journals, books, or websites from which you retrieve information. Peer-reviewed sources are typically more reliable*
9. __Citation Chaining__
	*Once you find a relevant article, check its references (citations) and look for more sources in those works. This is called citation chaining and can lead you to additional valuable materials*
10. __Keep an Annotated Bibliography__
	*As you find relevant sources, create an annotated bibliography that includes a summary of each source and how it relates to your research*
11. __Be Patient and Persistent__
	*Literature searches can be time-comsuming. Don't get discouraged if you don't find what you need right away. Keep refining your search strategy*

## Tips for Reading Neuroscientific Papers
### Anatomy of an Article
- __Title__: reading and understanding them can be useful
- __Journal (that the article was written for)__: The authors wrote the article with their journal audience in mind
- __Author List__
- __Abstract__: the summary/overview/general storyline that the paper will follow
- __Introduction__: The author's arguments/justification for the need for this work
- __Methods__: Detailed explanation of exactly how the study was conducted
  __Results__: Presentation fo results, often including tables and figures
  __Discussion__: Key conclusions, possible next steps and interpretation of results based on what they know about the literature and what this study contributes

### Steps for Reading an Article
1. Read the abstract and choose whether or not to continue
2. Skim the intro, noting potential follow up articles and key arguments
3. Skim the methods, noting the basic limitations and assumptions
4. Read the discussion, noting the main conclusions
5. Refer to the results to identify how the above conclusions were drawn. Pay close attention to the figures and their corresponding legends.
6. Take NOTES! Generate questions before, during and after the reading. Write down your questions and note key words / jargon to define.

>[!question] Important Questions To Ask As You Read
>1. What question are the authors testing?
>2. What is the hypothesis?
>3. Who are the subjects? What age range is being considered?
>4. What is the task or experimental protocol? What conditions are being compared?
>5. What techniques are the authors using to assess their dependent variable?
>6. What do the findings tell us about the relationship between the independent and dependent variable(s)?
>7. How do the findings relate to what you already know about this research area?
>8. How do the findings advance the field of knowledge?
>>[!important]
>> 9. Are the findings consistent with other studies? Are the authors' claims reasonable given the limitations of the techniques used? Do you think the task truly elicits the outcomes under investigation?
