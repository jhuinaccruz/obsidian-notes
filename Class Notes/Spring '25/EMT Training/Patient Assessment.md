## Scene Size Up
__Scene Size-Up__: The assessment of:
- Scene safety
- Wearing PPE/BSI
- Identifying the number of patients (triaging)
- Examining the Scene for MOI/NOI
- Identifying available resources and requesting additional assistance

## Primary Assessment

__Primary Assessment__: Once scene is safe, a primary assessment can be conducted to:
- Gather general impression and take spinal precautions
- Determine level of consciousness
- Determine chief complaint
- Determine the ABC's (ABCDE)
- Decide what method of transport is appropriate

__A primary assessment's main goal is to__: Assess and treat immediate life threats

__Any abnormal primary assessment requires__: ALS
### General Impression/C-Spine
__For general impressions, the most important information to gather is__:
- Stability (Stable/Questionable/Unstable)
- Appearance
- Work of Breathing
- Skin Color

__When first approaching the patient__:
- Make sure the patient sees you coming
- Address the patient by name
- Introduce yourself to the patient
- Ask about the chief complaint
- Assess for sighs of uncontrolled bleeding

__Uncontrolled external bleeding__: Generally suggested by a large amount of bleeding that is squirting, gushing, soaked through clothing, or is pooling under the patient, needs to be controlled before any other assessment

__When taking spinal precautions, it is important to assess__:
- MOI
- Whether manual stabilization is needed
- Whether complete immobilization is needed

### Level of Consciousness
__To assess levels of consciousness, we divide them into a hierarchy of four categories called__: AVPU (A simplification of the Glasgow Coma Scale)
1. Alert (most conscious)
2. Verbal (only able to communicate through unintelligible sound)
3. Painful (only able to communicate though reflex/pain response)
4. Unresponsive (least conscious)

__If the patient is alert, the patient should the be assessed for__: Orientation
- Person ("Can you tell me your name?")
- Place ("Can you tell me where you are?")
- Time ("What day is it today? What time is it?")
- Event ("Can you tell me why you called us today?")
### Chief Complaint
__Chief Complaint__: The patient's highest priority complaint is the chief complaint
### ABC(D)
__Determining the patient's ABCs means assessing__:
- AIrway function:
	- Patent: Open, clear, and maintained
	- Questionable
	- Nonpatent
- Breathing: Look for chest rise, listen for breathing sounds, feel for air
	- RRQ (Rate, rhythm, quality)
	- Skin condition
	- Trauma to the chest?
	- Oxygen Levels/Oxygenation
- Circulation:
	- RRQ
	- Skin quality
	- Bleeding? Trauma?
		- Blood Sweep
			- Head
			- Neck
			- Chest
			- Pelvis
			- Upper and Lower Extremities
- (Situationally when altered mental status: Disability)
	- Level of Consciousness
	- Glasgow Coma Scale

__Give high-concentration oxygen if__:
- Respirations are adequate but faster than 24 per minute or slower than 8 per minute
- Patient has difficulty breathing
- Patient shows signs of hypoxia

__Assist with ventilations if__:
- Patient is unable to breathe adequately
- Patient has injuries to the chest

__Breathing should assessed by__: Observing chest rise and by air expelled

### Rapid Trauma Exam
__Rapid Trauma Exam__: Performed to identify life threats and injuries, treating only life threats as you go
- Assess head by looking and feeling (DCAP-BTLS)
- Look at face (DCAP-BTLS)
- Assess Pupils
- Assess neck looking and feeling (DCAP-BTLS) for jugular Vein Distention (JVD), tracheal deviation, spinal step-off, vertebra that is not aligned with the rest
- Assess chest by looking and feeling (DCAP-BTLS) for chest wall movement, crepitus, subcutaneous emphysema, and paradoxical motion
- Ausculate for breath/lung sounds on both sides of chest
- Assess abdomen by looking and feeling (DCAP-BTLS), rigidity, and distention (four quadrants)
- Assess pelvis by looking and palpating (DCAP-BTLS), pressing inwards and downwards for instability, crepitus and tenderness
- Assess back and buttocks (DCAP-BTLS), feeling if necessary, and logrolling onto a backboard if necessary

__DCAP-BLTS__: Used to identify kinds of injuries
- Deformities
- Contusions
- Abrasions
- Punctures/Penetrations
- Burns
- Lacerations
- Tenderness
- Swelling

### Imminent Birth Questions*
__If there is any indication for imminent birth, ask__:
- Is your water broken?
	- When?
	- Any coloration to it?
- Are you currently experiencing any contractions?
	- When did they start?
	- How long did they last?
	- How far apart are they?
- Do you feel the need to push?

__If there is a yes to any of the three main imminent birth questions__: Perform a genital exam and prepare for birth if necessary
### Transport Decision
__Determining transport means assessing__:
- Whether immediate or delayed transport is needed
- Patient positioning/packaging
- Priority
- Destination
- ALS
## Secondary Asessment
__A secondary assessment determines__:
- A history of present illnesses/injuries
- Baseline vitals
- Focused assessment/Full Body Assessment
### Pregnancy History*
__Questions related to pregnancy history__:
- When was your last menstrual period?
- How many pregnancies have you had?
	- How many carried to full term?
- Have you received any prenatal care?
	- Any expected complications?
### HPI
__HPI__: History of Present Illnesses/Injuries

__If patient is unresponsive__: Patient information and clues about the incident may be obtained from:
- Family members present
- A person who may have witnessed the situation
- Bystanders
- Medical alert jewelry
- Other patient medical history documentation

__OPQRST__: Further assessed the patient's chief complaint
- __Onset__: "When did the problem start? Did it happen gradually/rapidly?"
- __Provocation/Palliation__: "What makes the problem better or worse?"
- __Quality__: "Describe what you're feeling"
- __Radiation/Region__: "Where is the pain?" "Does the pain move or hurt anywhere else?"
- __Severity__: on a scale of 0-10
	- Pain is on a scale of mild/moderate/severe
- __Time__: Acute/Chronic
### SAMPLE
__SAMPLE Questionnaire__: Used for a more broad history assessment
- __Signs/Symptoms__:
	- Loss of Consciousness?
	- Shortness of Breath?
	- Nausea?
	- Vomiting?
	- Diarrhea?
	- Headache?
	- Chest pain?
	- Dizziness?
	- Tingling?
	- Weakness?
- __Allergies__: (Food, medication, environmental factors?) "What happens when you are exposed to _ ?"
- __Medication__
	- "What medications do you take?"
	- "What are they for?"
	- "Have you taken them as prescribed?"
	- "Have there been any recent changes?"
- __Past Medical History__
	- "Any medical conditions?"
	- "Any recent surgeries? Hospitalizations?"
	- "Any recent illnesses or injuries?"
- __Last Oral Intake__ "Last thing you ate/drank? When?"
- __Events leading up to the Chief Complaint__ "What do you think happened?"
### Baseline Vitals
__Baseline Vital Collecting includes__:
- Respiration Rate
- Pulse
- Blood Pressure
- Pulse Oximetry
- Blood Glucose
- Temperature
### Focused Physical Assessment
__Focused Physical Assessments focus on__: Relevant systems and regions to the patient's chief complaint
### Full Trauma/Full Body Exam
__Full Trauma Exam__: Systematic examination of the entire body, identifying injuries not found during the rapid scan.
- It should be performed on all patients who have sustained a significant MOI, had a previously conducted rapid trauma scan, is unconscious, or in an otherwise critical condition
- See, hear, and feel in that order when doing a physical assessment)
1. Observe face (DCAP-BTLS)
2. Visually check eyes for redness/contact lenses
3. Check pupils
4. Visually check behind ears for battle signs, drainage
	1. Perform Halo Test
5. Palpate head (DCAP-BTLS)
6. Palpate facial bones (DCAP-BTLS) for crepitus, deviation, depression, and abnormal mobility
7. Check nostrils for drainage
8. Palpate mandible (DCAP-BTLS) for crepitus, deviation, depression, abnormal mobility
9. Check mouth for blood, vomit, broken teeth, trauma to the tongue, or other airway obstructions
10. Visually inspect and palpate neck (DCAP-BTLS) for jugular venous distension, tracheal deviation, and subcutaneous emphysema
11. Palpate cervical spine (DCAP-BTLS) for step-offs, midline pain, and maintain C-spine alignment if necessary
12. Inspect the chest for equal chest rise, paradoxical motion, guarding
13. Palpate the chest, shoulders, clavicles, ribs, and sternum (DCAP-BTLS) for crepitus and tenderness, avoiding previously-established bruises/fractures
14. Compare mid-clavicular and mid-axillary breathing sounds
15. Visually inspect and palpate the abdomen (DCAP-BTLS) for distention, rigidity, rebound tenderness, and pulsating masses
16. Visually inspect and palpate the pelvis (DCAP-BTLS) for instability
17. Visually inspect and palpate genitalia (DCAP-BTLS) for incontinence, bleeding, and priapism (men)
18. Visually inspect and palpate extremities (DCAP-BTLS), focusing on long bones, joints, knees, bones in foot, elbow, hands, and assess CSMs in each extremity
19. Check posterior (DCAP-BTLS), palpate the scapla, posterior ribs, and each vertebrae and for incontinence

__A trauma exam is used over a focused exam when__: There is any evidence of any MOI
### APGAR*
__APGAR__: Used to evaluate children 1, 5, and 10 minutes after birth, on a scale from 0-2:
- Appearance
- Pulse
- Grimace
- Activity
- Respiration

__APGAR scores should be (after 1 and 5/10 minutes)__: 7/8 and 9-10 respectively
## Reassessment
__Parts of a reassessment__:
- Reassess the chief complaint, monitor any changes
- Redo primary assessment
- Recheck vital signs
- Recheck interventions/treatments
- Reassess transport decision
