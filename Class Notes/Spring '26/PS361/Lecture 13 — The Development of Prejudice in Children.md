>*Children are not born prejudiced, but become aware of social categories very early in life*

__Categorization__: Helps children organize the social world and predict behavior
- Categorization in children manifests itself in behaviors such as
	- Ingroup preference
	- Expectations about groups
	- Belief about group differences

__Kite et. al.__ (2022): Children of different categories are shown a doll and asked to categorize it into separate categories, as well as shown pictures of people and asked to sort them into groups
- *Results*: Children develop explicit awareness of social categories gradually
	- 3-4 years old: Gender
	- 4-5 years old: Major racial categories
	- 5-9 years old: Other racial groups
	- 9-11 years old: Non-visible categories

Awareness is split into two different kinds: implicit and explicit

__Habituation Paradigm__: Participants are shown images of the same category in the *Habituation Phase*, then shown images of a different category in the *Testing Phase*
- The test lies in measuring fixation time: low fixation time means the stimuli was categorized as similar to the images in the *Habituation Phase*; a higher fixation time means the stimuli is recognized as new

__Quinn et. al.__ (2002, 2016): Infants were placed in a habituation paradigm
- *Results*: Infants can distinguish between gender and age
	- Additionally, they show preferences for physically attractive faces, as well as familiar faces
- *Conclusions*: Infants are sensitive to visual differences and begin forming basic social categories early o
	- Gender/age perception emerges early, consistently, and encodes strong, reliable preferences

__Kelly et. al.__ (2007): Infants (White, 3-9 months old) were placed in a habituation paradigm, testing for specific discrimination between ethnic groups
- *Results*: Infants distinguish between races
	- The three month olds distinguish faces from different racial groups
	- Six- and nine-month olds show an own race recognition advantage
- *Conclusion*: Infants have a predisposition to exposure familiarity
	- However, these do not imply prejudice

>*Infants detect racial differences...this does not yet reflect bias/prejudice*

>*Race perception is initially flexible, becoming shaped by experience and showing perceptual narrowing over time*

>*Infants are powerful pattern detectors. Social categories begin as perceptual distinctions, but bias is not present at birth — it develops through experience and social context*

>*Social category identification is studied in children using classification and sorting tasks*

>*Awareness of group differences is part of development; prejudice involves the evaluation of said groups*

__Clark and Clark__ (1947): Also known as the Classic Doll Studies; participants (about 250 Black children across all studies, ages 3—7 years old from segregated schools) were shown two dolls differing in skin color and asked to assign them traits (good/bad)
- Participants had to also identify which doll looked like them
- *Results*: Children preferred the white doll, attributing the positive traits to the White doll and negative ones to the Black doll
- *Limitations*:
	- Limited and inconsistent control groups
		- Results across studies are mixed
	- Small sample sizes in studies
	- Forced-Choice format (forced binary choice)
	- Confounding variables
		- The Black dolls were simply "uglier" due to manufacturing errors
	- Interpretation bias
		- Children in said development stage choose the kind of doll based on the kind of people they encounter more of; choosing White more is a sign of the perceptual salience stage of development rather than "self-hatred"

__Byrd et. al.__ (2017): A replication of the Classic Doll Study; participants (mostly Black) were shown dolls differing in skin tone and hair texture and asked to evaluate them based on who was nicer, meaner, pretty, etc.
- In this replication, the participants could also refrain from assigning values to the dolls
- *Results*: Mixed across all categories
- *Conclusions*: Children are innately aware of complex social hierarchies corresponding to beauty norms, skin tones, status

>*Children are exposed to these messages through their social environments*

>*Prejudice is very low in young majority-group children, increasing around ages 5-7; later development of prejudice (or lack thereof) depends on intergroup contact*

__Typical Development Pattern for Minority Group Children__:
- 2-4: Somewhat positive attitudes toward majority-group members
	- Later, there is an increasing awareness of status differences between groups

>*In biracial children, prejudice decreases since they might identify with both groups*
