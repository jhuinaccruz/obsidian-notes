>*Children are not born prejudiced, but they do become aware of social categories very early in life*

__Categorization helps children__:
- Organize the social world
- Predict behavior
- Understand group membership

__Once categories exist, children may begin to show__:
- Ingroup preference
- Expectations about groups
- Beliefs about groups differences

__Kite et. al., 2022__: Chlidren become aware of social categories early in development through two different awareness'
- __Habituation Paradigm__: If attention increases (staring in babies for example), the subject recognizes the object as infant
- The study shoes that infants can distinguish gender and age
	- Also show preference for physically attractive faces
- Explicit categorization was tested through classification tasks
	- *Task*: Children are given pictures of people and asked to divide people into categories
		- *Findings*: Children develop explicit awareness of social categories gradually
			- 3-4 years: Gender
			- 4-5 years: Major racial categories
			- 5-9 years: Other racial groups
			- 9-11 years: Non-visible categories
- *Conclusion*: The ability to form social categories emerges very early on

>*Awareness of group differences is normal development*


__Clark and Clark__ (1947): Black children from segregated schools are shown two dolls that differ by skin doll; asked to assign traits to and self-identify with the doll
- Many children
	- Preferred the white doll
	- Attributed positive traits to the White doll and negative ones the Black dolls
- *Limitations*:
	- Limited/inconsistent control group
	- Small sample sizes
	- Forced-choice format
	- Interpretation of results

>*Revisiting the doll paradigm has found more complex patterns (results across studies are mixed; children's responses often reflect awareness of social hierarchies*

__Byrd et al.__ (2017): 50 (mostly Black) children are shown dolls (Clark & Clark replication)
- *Findings*: When given the choice for ugly/good doll, most said none/did not choose
	- When asked which hair was the prettiest, the doll with the straightest hair was considered the prettiest

__Kelly et. al.__ (2007):
- Found race perception is initially flexible but becomes shaped by experience, reflecting a narrowing perception over time
