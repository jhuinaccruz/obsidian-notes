>*Medicine is expected to be scientific and unbiased, yet it is still affected by human biases*

__Tuskegee Study__ (1932 - 1972): 600 Black men were misled about a syphilis study
- Effective treatment was withheld

>*Past injustices (and current ones) have eroded trust in medical institutions, causing imbalances in care seeking and treatment adherence*

>*Patients with similar conditions may receive different diagnoses, treatments, and overall levels of care not always explained by medical need*

>*Black patients may present with more severe symptoms and therefore less likely to receive life-saving procedures, with similar patterns observed across other diseases*

__Bias in Treatment Decisions__: There is evidence of biases in interactions with Black patients
- Implicitly, patients may give lower satisfaction scores, citing subtle differences in care
- Explicitly, it may manifest itself in patients having less involvement in the decision-making of their own care

>*Some providers falsely believe Black patients have higher pain tolerance due to biological differences in sensitivity, leading to Black patients have their pain underestimated and receiving weaker medications even when their symptoms are worse*

>*Women are perceived to be more emotional, hysterical, and exaggerative in their pain, where male counterparts are seen as more stoic, rational, and less expressive of pain*

>*Women report more frequent, severe, and longer lasting pain, yet are less likely to be taken seriously, and thus receive lesser pain treatments, instead having symptoms attributed to psychological causes*

>*In practice, women wait longer for pain treatments (such as pain medications), instead administrating sedatives. Additionally, women are less likely to receive diagnostic testing.*

__Intersectionality of Pain Assessment In Women__:
- *The Double Burden* and *Racial Myths*: Women of color suffer from the intersectionality of being both a woman (seen as hysteric/emotional) and a racial minority (long-standing medical bias of seeing women of color as more pain-tolerant), resulting in less-than-adequate pain relief
- *Weight Bias*: Overattribution of symptoms to weight leading to delayed critical diagnostic scans happens especially for women

>*Research gaps from the "default male" in medical curricula and research leads to implicit biases and a lack of protocols for women especially*

__Psychological Mechanisms that contribute to biases__:
- *Perception under Uncertainty*: In situations with unknowns, people implicitly fill in the blanks based on primitive categorizations
- *Attribution of Causes*: Similar behavior is attributed differently based on race/sex/gender
- *Stereotype-based Expectations*

__SIgnal Detection Theory__: A mathematical framework composed of two main factors
- *Sensitivity* ($d'$): The ability of distinguishing *signal* (physical pain) from *noise* (emotional distress)
- *Criterion* ($\beta$): The threshold of action

>*Lower sensitivity is seen for outgroup patients; sterotypes result in different (higher) criterion*

>*Bias in healthcare providers increases when providers are rushed, stressed, or overloaded*
