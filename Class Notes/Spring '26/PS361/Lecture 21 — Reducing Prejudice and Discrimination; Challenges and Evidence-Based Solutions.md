__Why is prejudice so sticking in our conscience?__:
- Awareness *does not inherently* lead to change
- Bias can operate at multiple levels
- Efforts to reduce bias can have the opposite effects

>*Prejudices activate quickly in automatic processes, whereas in controlled processes they act slower due to their rooting in beliefs, values, and intentions. These two processes often disagree with one another, especially in "low-prejudice individuals"*

>*Controling bias is difficult because automatic processes occur without intent, where cognitive load is required to combat these processes is often reduced due to real-world demands. In other words, bias control takes effort and motivation, both in short demand for most people*

__Strategies for Controlling Bias__:
- *Suppressing biased thoughts*: Requires active monitoring to maintain
	- *Rebound Effect*: A slip in monitoring can result in increased biases

__Macrae et. al.__ (1994): Participants were asked to write a day-in-the-life essay provided an image of a male "Skinhead"; one group was asked to avoid using stereotypes
- Participants then also wrote a second essay with no restrictions, then got to meet said person
- *Results*: In the first essay, the group told to avoid stereotypes followed instructions, writing less stereotypes than the other group
	- However, in the second essay, the same group wrote significantly more in the way of stereotypes than their counterparts
	- When meeting the person, the same group chose to sit farther away from him than the other group
- *Conclusions*: Repression can lead to the same biases rebounding stronger as soon as attentional resources change
	- __White Bear Problem__: Asking people not to think of a bear results in the bear being primed, such that as soon as one gives up "not" thinking of the bear, the bear becomes primed to be thought of

__Why do people resist belief change?__
- Cognitive dissonance
- Threat to identity when beliefs are attached to self-concept
- Defensive rejection and rationalizing of information to fit existing beliefs
- Perceived threats to autonomy leading to reactance to assert freedom

>*Belief change resistance can result in increased negative attitudes*

__Intergroup Contact Theory__ (Contact Hypothesis): Coined by Gordon Allport in 1954; under the right conditions, contact between groups of people can reduce bias altogether.
- The four main criteria are
	- Equal status
	- Common Goals
	- intergroup cooperation
	- Support of authorities, law, and norms
- Results in reduced anxieties as well as increased empathy and individuation

>*Intergroup contact reliably reduces prejudice across multiple groups regardless of perfect conditions or otherwise*

__Extended Contact__: Simply knowing someone who has an outgroup friend can reduce prejudice indirectly

__Imagined Contact__: Imagining positive intergroup contact can reduce anxiety and improve expecations
- Effects are smaller compared to actual intergroup contact; context-dependent

__Common Ingroup Identity Model__: Bias can be reduced by redefining group boundaries to include others into a more superordinate identity; "Us vs Them" into "We"
- Can be achieved institutionally through school, media, and policy; can also be inhibited institutionally

__Paradoxical Intervention__: Rather than arguing against prejudice directly, use paradoxical thinking (extrapolate and exaggerate the "flawed" logic rather than refute it outright)

__What makes interventions effective?__:
- Social norms (strongest effects)
- Intergroup contact (consistent effects)
- Perspective taking (moderate)
- Cognitive Retraining (limited lasting effects)
