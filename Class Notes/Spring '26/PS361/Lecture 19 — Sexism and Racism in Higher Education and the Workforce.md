>*Women are especially underrepresented in STEM; receiving less bachelors, masters, and PhDs*

>*Women represent half the total U.S. college-educated workforce, yet only 28% of the STEM workforce*

__Moss-Racusin et. al.__ (2012): Participant professors, in a double-blind experiment, were asked to evaluate the applications of undergraduates applying to a fictional lab manager position
- The applications were exactly the same except for differing (a man's name versus a woman's name)
- *Results*: The man was consistently chosen over the female candidate, despite being the exact same application
	- Participants showed more willingness to invest time and mentor the male applicant
	- The starting salary provided was higher for the male applicant than the female one
	- However, the female was seen as more likeable, yet it did not translate favorably otherwise
	- Both male and female professors exhibited the same biases
- *Conclusions*: Social factors exist in the way of STEM positions and women
	- Said factors and biases can affect both men and women

__Starr et. al.__ (2022): A longitudinal study examining children and the intersectionality between gender, race, ethnicity, and outcomes in life
- *Results*: Parents consistently pushed boys towards math, asserting boys are better at math than girls
	- This occurred primarily in White families
- *Conclusion*: Parents reinforce traditional gender stereotypes and self-concepts (related to math)

__Stereotype Threat__: A disruptive concern that one will be evaluated based on a negative stereotype

__Spencer et. al.__ (1999): Participants took the GRE; one group of test-takers were told that men typically do better than women, the other group was told that there were no gender differences in results
- *Results*: Women's results suffered when told of the "gender disparity"
	- However, in the other group, women's scores increased, matching the men's
- *Conclusion*: Exam results can be attributed in part to psychological pressure of the stereotype threat

__Aronson et. al.__ (1999): Participants took a math exam; one group was told that Asians performed above-average, the other group was told nothing
- *Result*: White test-takers average dropped between groups
- *Conclusion*: Stereotype threat can affect everybody, not just minorities

__Shih et. al.__ (1999): Female Asian students took an exam after taking a priming test
- Participants were asked either about their Asian identity (*Asian identity priming*), female identity (*Female identity priming*), or not at all
- *Results*: Performance was affected based on what identity was primed and salient
- *Conclusion*: Intersectionality effects depend on which identity is salience

>*Stereotype threat effects are considered invalid by some*

__Stereotype Threat Effect Criticisms__:
- Other existing theories and explanations are simpler
- Publication bias: Positive-aligning studies are pubished; contradictory studies are not
- Stereotype threat effects are overestimated in the real world

>*A lot of social psychology is undergoing a "Replication Crisis"*

__Quadlin__ (2018): Men got callbacks for jobs proportional to their GPA, whereas women did not have the same linear relationship
- For men applicants, competence mattered over anything, whereas for women it was likeability that mattered most

__Boucher & Murphy__ (2017): Women with similar grades and achievements felt less belonging in their positions in STEM than their male counterparts

__Dovidio & Gaertner__ (2002): Participants judged potential admissions candidates; candidates varied in race and credentials
- *Results*: Qualified candidates showed little bias between race
	- As qualifications became more mixed in quality, biases emerged towards White candidates
	- Comparing 1989 results to those a decade later, researchers found that implicit biases remained the same despite "social progress"

>*Asian job candidates were twice as likely to receive a call back if they whitened their resumes with "White" names*

__Milkman et. al.__ (2014): 6,500 professors received emails from prospective students; students varied in race and gender
- *Results*: Faculty ignored requests from women and minorities at a higher rate than similar counterparts
