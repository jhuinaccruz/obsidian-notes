__How do you study information processing between stimuli__:
- Introspection
- Behaviorism
- Neural reductionism

__Neurons__: About 80-100 billion in the brain, engaging in electrocommunication between neurons and other cells of the body

__Afferent Signaling__: Receiving information from sensory inputs/organs
__Efferent Signaling__: Sending out electric signals to muscular groups for movement

__Methods for measuring a neuron's activity__:
- Recording action potentials' voltage spikes

__Simple Cell__: Located in the visual cortex, it responds specifically to an edge at a particular location and a particular angle, containing an
- Excitatory region, where light in this part of the visual field increases the neuron's activity
- Inhibitory region: Where light in this part of the visual field suppresses the neuron's activity

__Grandmother cell__: A theory where there is a cell in the brain specifically for identifying specific people, for example one's own grandmother

>*Parts of the brain respond to both simple and complex visual features*

>*The computational function of a neuron isn't always this obvious from observing its activity. More often, you find mixed selectivity*

__Mixed Selectivity__: A neuron's ability to be specifically activated based on specific subsets of pattern
- Raises the larger question as to why/how such selectivity is constructed

__Levels to abstract neuron function__:
- Computation: What is the goal of computation?
- Algorithmic: What is the representation for the input/output, and what is the algorithm work?
- Implementation: How can computation and the algorithm itself be represented?

>*Each level puts "soft" constraints on the others*
