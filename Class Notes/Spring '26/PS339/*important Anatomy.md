__Primary Somatosensory Cortex__: Posterior to the Central Sulcus

__Primary Auditory Cortex__: Located in Heschl's Gyrus; within the Lateral Sulcus
- On the superior surface of the Temporal Lobe

__Motor Map Homunculus__: Located on the precentral gyrus

__Sensory Map Homunculus__: Located on the postcentral gyrus

__Facial Recognition Network__:
- Occipital Face Area: Located in the inferior occipital gyrus; recognize individual parts of a face
- Fusiform Face Area: Located on the ventral surface of the temporal lobe; responsible for recognizing faces
- Superior Temporal Sulcus: Processes changing aspects of a face
- Amygdala: Adds emotional significance to recognized faces

__Object Recognition Pathways__: Starts in the Primary Visual Cortex (V1)
- Ventral: Travels down the Temporal Lobe into the
	- Lateral Occipital Complex
	- Fusiform Gyrus
- Dorsal: Travels up the Parietal Lobe into the
	- Intraparietal Sulcus

__Attention Pathways__:
- Dorsal Attention Network: Bilateral, focused in the
	- Frontal Eye Fields: Control voluntary eye movements
	- Intraparietal Sulcus: Maintains a spatial priority map
- Ventral Attention Network: Right-hemisphere dominant, focused in the
	- Temporoparietal Junction: Attention-breaker
	- Ventral Frontal Cortex (Inferior Frontal Gyrus)

__Spatial Attention Mechanisms__:
- Pulvinar (in the Thalamus): Filters what visual information gets sent to the cortex and what doesn't
- Superior Colliculus: Midbrain structure that coordinated involuntary overt attention
- Lateral Intraparietal Area: Neurons fire stronger when a stimulus relevant to the goal

__Hemispatial Neglect__: Occuring due to damage in the Right Parietal Lobe
- __Egocentric__: Related to damage to the Intraparietal Lobe/Temporoparietal Junction
- __Object-Centered__: Related to damage to the Superior Temporal Gyrus

__Salience Map__: Primarily located in the Intraparietal Sulcus

__Priority Map__: Similar to the Salience Map + anatomically relevant to the Lateral Intraparietal Area

__Population Vectors__: M1 neurons are broadly tuned; the Superior Colliculus look at populations of them, sums them up as "votes", then finds the strongest vote to determine direction of the vector

__Cerebellum__: Its primary functions lie in
- Fine-tuning motor control
- Classical Conditioning such as
	- Eye-Blinking reflex
- Damage leads to Ataxia

__Basal Ganglia__: The gatekeeper of the motor cortex, deciding what is allowed to happen through its pathways, critical for habit formation
- Its parts include
	- The Striatum (from which it receives its input): Translates motivation into action
		- Caudate
		- Putamen
	- Putamen: Outer portion
	- Globus Pallidus: Inner Portion
	- Subthalamic Nucleus: Target for DBS in Parkinson's
	- Substantia Nigra
		- Pars Compacta: Where dopamine is produces
		- Pars Reticulata: Outputs to the Superior Colliculus

__Go/No-Go Pathways__: Inhibits and allows for movement by disinhibiting the thalamus
- Direct: Initiates movement
	1. The cortex plans the movement
	2. The Striatum receives excitatory signals from the cortices, sending inhibitory signals (GABA) to the...
	3. Internal Globus Pallidus and the Substantia Nigra, which effectively disinhibits the...
	4. Thalamus, allowing it to excite and cause activity in the Motor Cortices and Superior Colliculus
- Inidirect: Inhibits movement
	1. The cortex excites a different area in the striatum
	2. The striatum sends inhibitory signals to the...
	3. External Globus Pallidus, whose inhibition affects the...
	4. Subthalamic Nucleus, since it is kept inhibited by the External Globus Pallidus. Its excitation in turn sends excitatory signals to the...
	5. Internal Globus Pallidus and Substantia Nigra, which inhibits the Thalamus even more

__Hippocampus__: Its function is essential for declarative memory, which decreases the more memories age (Standard Consolidation)
- In recent memories, the hippocampus is almost exclusively relied on to maintain connections between it and cortical areas corresponding to the retrieval said memory.
- For older memories, consolidation in critical cortical areas make it so the hippocampus relies less on the hippocampus for its retrieval

__Procedural Memory__: Also known as non-declarative; relies on anatomy such as;
- Basal Ganglia: Habit formation and procedural learning
- Cerebellum: Critical for fine tuning motor movements and balance
- Motor Cortex

__Working Memory__: Relies on critical anatomy such as;
- Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex: Keeps semantic information looping and "in working memory"
- Parietal Cortex: Represent spatial working memory
- Secondary Visual/Auditory Areas: Briefly hold sensory-specific memory (mental images/echo of a voice) in working memory

__Episodic Memory__: Also known as declarative memory; relies on
- Hippocampus: Encodes new episodic memories
- Entorhinal Cortex: Information intermediary between the hippocampus and the rest of the brain
- Thalamus: Consolidation and retrieval of episodic information
- Cortical Areas: Long-term episodic memory retention

__Broca's Aphasia__: Caused by damage to the Left Inferior Frontal Gyrus

__Wernicke's Aphasia__: Caused by damage to the posterior part of the Left Superior Temporal Gyrus

__Sign Language Aphasias__:
- Broca's (Expressive): Slow signing, heavy, and grammatically broken
- Wernicke's: Fluid, but incorrect and/or meaningless

__Visual Word Form Area__: Specialized region in the Left Fusiform Gyrus

__Anterior Cingulate Cortex__: Its main role is to conflict monitor and detect errors
- Also processes emotional pain and values how much it hurts (in ourselves and others)

__Striatum__: Intermediary between the reward system and its motor output
- Received dopaminergic input from the Ventral Tegmental Area
	- Processes the difference between expected and actual rewards
- Shifts behavior between the Caudate (goal-oriented) to the Putamen (habit-oriented)

__Mirror Neuron Network__: Relies on
- Inferior Frontal Gyrus: Goal-related planning of actions
- Ventral Premotor Cortex: Organizes/executes motor sequences
- Inferior Parietal Lobe: Encodes "how" an action will be executed
- Superior Temporal Sulcus: Provides visual input of biological motion

__Right Temporoparietal Junction__: Main component of Theory of Mind processes

__Biological Motion__: Relies on
- Superior Temporal Sulcus: Differentiates between movement of an inanimate object and animate beings

__Empathy__: A dual process system involving anatomy such as
- Affective (Emotional): Automatic process
	- Anterior Insula: Mirrors negative states
	- Anterior Cingulate Cortex: Processes emotional distress
- Cognitive: Perspective taking
	- Medial Prefrontal Cortex: Involved in mentalization processes
	- Temporoparietal Junction
