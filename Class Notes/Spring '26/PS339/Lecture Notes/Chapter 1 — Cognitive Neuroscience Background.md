__Cognitive Neuroscience__: Combines a cognitive approach to the mind with neuroscience
- Genes and experience equally determine behavior
- Focuses on representation to mental information and algorithms for mental computations

>*The mind is an emergent property of the brain*

__Cognitive Psychology__: Emphasizes information processing in the mind and biological endowments/limitations of the mind

__Behaviorism__: Focuses on associations between environment and observable behavior
- Internal thoughts are 'mere speculation'

__Cognitivism__: Abilities are innate, the brain is made up of complex models and structures

__Barlow's Neuron Doctrine__: A bottom-up approach that says that the nervous system can be understood by a selective firing of neurons
- David Marr's Critique: Neurons are only a part of the overall "algorithm"

__Marr's Three Levels__: Provides a framework on how information processing must be understood
- *Computational Theory*: What does a process do and why does it do it?
- *Representation and Algorithm*: How are the inputs, outputs, and intermediate states be represented, and how is the input transformed into the output?
- *Hardware Implementation*: How is the algorithm represented and executed with different technologies?

>*Cognitive neuroscience can focus on computational theory, representation, and algorithm without worrying about implementation limitations*

__Representation of Information__: A formal system for making certain information explicit at the expense of obscuring other information

__What constitutes an answer in neuroscience?__:
- Localization: Where in the brain does the function reside
- Computation: What algorithms is being used for a cognitive function?
- Representation: How is the object viewed as in a cognitive function?
- Implementation: How does our neural circuitry implement these computations and representations?
