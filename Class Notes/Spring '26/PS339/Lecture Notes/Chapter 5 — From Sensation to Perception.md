__Sensation__: Mental experiences driven by external stimuli

__Perception__: The set of processes by which we recognize, organize, and make sense of the sensation we receive from environmental stimuli

>*Perception utilizes stored knowledge and makes unconscious inferences*

__Checker Shadow Illusion__: The light check in the shadow is the same grey as a dark check in the bright area

__Senses beyond the 5__:
- Equilibrioception: Sense of balance
	- Relies on the vestibular system, as well as vision and proprioception
		- Proprioception: Unconscious ability to perceive position, movement, and spatial orientation using sensory receptors in muscles, joints, and the inner ear
		- There is no primary vestibular cortex (there is evidence for parietal)
		- Vestibular signals are mixed with other sensory cortical networks that make up equilibrioception

 __Olfactory Perception__:
 - Specificity+Combinatorial Coding onto 5500 glomeruli
 - No relay through thalamus, but rather olfactory cortex

__Gustatory Perception__:
- 5 Types of chemical receptors
	- Sweet
	- Salty
	- Sour
	- Bitter
	- Umami
- Sensitivity varies across the tongue
- Connects to the
	- Medulla's gustatory nucleus
	- Thalamus: Ventral Posterior Medial Nucleus
	- Anterior Insula (Primary Gustatory Cortex)
		- Connects to caudal orbitofrontal cortex (reward circuitry)

__Somatosensation__:
- 4 primary types of input
	- Touch
		- Merkel cells for regular touch
		- Meissner's cells for light touch
		- Pacinian cells for pressure sensation
	- Temperature (Ruffini cells)
	- Pain
	- Proprioception

__Pathway to somatosensory__:
1. Touch receptors detect stimulation of the skin and generate action potentials
2. The axons of the receptors enter the dorsal horn of the spinal cord and synapse on spinal neurons
3. The first synapse of the ascending column is made in the medulla
4. Output from the medulla crosses to innervate the contralateral thalamus and from there projects to the somatosensory cortex

__Homunculus__: A distorted map of the body on the brain's cortex representing sensory (postcentral gyrus) or motor (precentral gyrus) nerve distribution

>*Cortical area reflects sensitivity of species*

>*Somatosensory cortical response increases with years in training*

__Phantom Limb__: After the amputation of a limb, the brain's representation of a once-existing limb persists

__Cochlea__:
- As sound travels down to the apex, so does the frequency perceived

__Sound Localization__: Compares signals from both ears through inter-aural intensity/level differences (ILD) and time differences (ITD)
- Computed in the brainstem
- ITD occurs in the Medial Superior Olive
- ILD occurs in the Lateral superior olive

__Vision Photoreceptors__:
- Rods
- Cones

__Lens__: focuses images onto the retina
__Fovea__: Has the largest concentration of photoreceptors, especially cones

__Trichromats__: A typical person that can see 2.3 million collors
__Dichromats__: Also known as colorblindness, when either a red or green cone type is mutated or missing

__Acuity__: Determined by how retinal receptors are distributed

__Optic Chiasm__: Where optic fibers separate

__Homonymous Hemianopia__: When occipital lobe damage occurs in one hemisphere, blindness occurs in the opposite field of both eyes

__Geniculostriate Pathway__: The main path for vision, responsible for conscious, high-resolution experience to the world
1. Retina
2. Lateral Geniculate Nucleus
3. Visual Cortex (V1)

__Tectopulvinar Pathway__: The backdoor for vision, evolutionarily developed for fast and unconscious responses
1. Retina
2. Superior Colliculus
3. Pulvinare
4. Extrastriate Cortex (V2-5/MT)

__Blindsight__: Occurs when there is damage to the primary visual cortex, such that subjects are not aware of visual stimuli, yet can still respond to them
-  This occurs because it bypasses the typical route

__Hypercolumns__: Neurons grouped in a column based on their preference for orientation, eye dominance and spatial location, mapped orderly across surface
- When you get to the edge of the orientation columns, you reach the secondary visual cortex

__Maps of the visual cortex__: Over two dozen visual maps
- As you move away from "optimal dot receptive fields", the receptive fields become more complex as they go up the hierarchy (V2+) and then go into two different pathways
	- Dorsal into the parietal lobe: Where/how? (space guidance and motor planning, unconscious)
	- Ventral into the temporal lobe: What? (recognition, conscious)
- V4: In the ventral occipito-temporal region, responds to colorful stimuli
- MT: In the lateral posterior-temporal region, reponds to moving stimuli
>*Color is more selectively perceived by neurons than motion*

__Fasciculus__: Major white matter fiber bundle

__Achromatopsia__: Cortical color blindness due to damage to V4
- Rare; 1 in 30000

__Akinetopsia__: Cortical motion blindness, where perception is reported as 'still frames'
- Extremely rare
- Occurs when there is large bilateral damage to the MT

__Multi-Sensory Regions__: Regions combine multiple sensations, such that they work together

__Synesthesia__: Highly specific percepts associated with stimuli that do not contain these elements
- Very from person to person
- May occur when there is extra connections between white matter

__Inverse Optics Problem__: Recovering structure from the external world while also the understanding the conditions in which you are viewing
- Many scene conditions (outputs) are consistent with inputs, such that there is no single unique solution

__Luminance__: Light as a product of the reflectance (shade) of the visible surface and the illumination (light source) falling on the surface
- In the inverse optics problem, the eyes receives luminance but makes up unreceived reflectance

__Size Constancy__: The same projection onto the retina could be produced by an infinite pairing of size and distance of an object

__Perceptual Ambiguity__: Shading information such as luminance gradients can be used to infer object shape, such that it makes things ambiguous between convex or concave lighting

__After Image Illusion__: A percept that can be seen even after the stimulus has disappeared

__fMRI of Aftereffects__:
1. Adaptation: Neurons tune to the stimulus they are watching, eventually adapting and putting in less effort and reducing activity
2. Aftereffect: The stimulus stops or changes and the neurons immediately rebound with activity
- The resulting paradox is that even though nothing is going on, the neurons are active, which creates the illusion of an aftereffect
