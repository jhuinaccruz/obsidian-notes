__Domain-Specific Processing__: Specialized processing limited to a specific type of information
- A fast and largely unconscious process
- Congruent with the idea that we have (evolutionarily) developed specific mind modules

__Domain-General Processing__: Broad general-purpose processing
- Slow and attentionally demanding

>*Cognitive control functions are largely domain-general*

>*The human neocortex is about 1000x the surface area of the mouse's neocortex, yet only twice the thickness*

>*White matter expansion is substantial in humans relative to other primates*

>*The prefrontal cortex in humans is also larger relative to other mammals*

__Cognitive Control Network__: Areas of the brain that support cognitive processing
- Includes the lateral prefrontal and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, as well as the lateral/medial posterior parietal lobe and the anterior insula, etc etc.

__Executive Control Regions__:
- Prefrontal cortex
- Orbitofrontal cortex
- Anterior cingulate
- Posterior parietal cortex
- Caudate/Putamen (Striatum)
- Parts of the cerebellum

__Basal Ganglia__: Responsible for different parts of motor functions
- The anterior caudate works in the prefrontal loop

__Working Memory__: A function of goal representations and activation/maintenance of long-term knowledge
- Where information is maintained and manipulated
- Not only located in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, but decoded across a broad network of brain structures

__Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex__: Implicated in working memory processes
- Findings show there are specific neurons encoding for what-where, and what-where

__Delay Period Activity__: A working-memory paradigm measuring how neurons fire even in the absence of the presented stimuli
- Firing neurons implicate a working memory loop keeping said neurons firing

>*In a delay period activity with faces, the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex shows a greater delay period than the fusiform face area, where its activity increases proportional to the number of faces*

__N-Back Task__: Participants are shown stimuli and asked to compare $n$-stimuli ago
- 1-back is a simple working memory comparison, increasing in complexity as $n$ grows

__Goal Directed Behavior__: Can be represented as a hierarchical tree of plannings
- Anatomically manifested in how the lateral prefrontal cortex fires for more complex tasks from the posterior (caudal) to the anterior (rostral)

__Perseveration__: Continued repeated behavior without the cognitive ability to "stop"
- Damage to the lateral prefrontal cortex

__Response Inhibition__:

__Rule Switching Task__: Measures how prefrontal lobe lesions may affect and cause perseveration
- Cards of three dimensions (number, number of shapes, and color) are placed in front of a patient
	- The participant then (through some trial and error) matches a new card to the existing cards
	- Once the participant understands the rule, experimenters change the rules, and the process repeats of trial-and-error matching
- *Expected Results*: For patients with frontal lobe lesions, they are able to learn the rules at first, but get stuck with the same set of rules even when changed
	- For a similar task (Wisconsin Card Sorting Task), the interior frontal sulcus shows increased activity for rule switching tasks

__Cognitive Set__: The rules of a task held in working memory
- An inability to manipulate a cognitive set is a sign of dysexecutive symptoms and more broadly, frontal lobe lesion

__Environmental Dependency Syndrome__: A failure to inhibit behaviors that are contextually inappropriate
- __Utilization Behavior__: Grabbing objects in view and start an object based on said object
- Implies that stimuli take over cognitive control over behavior, such that stimuli drives behavior

__Norman and Shallice Model of Response Selection__ (Supervisory Attentional System): The perceptual system directly activates action schemas, kept in check by inhibitory control systems
- The two main inhibitory controls are
	- *Contention Scheduling*: Inhibition between competing action schemas to focus on one at a time
	- *Supervisory Attentional System*: A top-down process where cognition flexibly controls goals, overriding all other systems

__Anterior Cingulate Cortex__ (ACC): Located on the medial surface of the frontal lobe; primarily involved in higher order cognitive control processes, as well as
- Conflict resolution
- Monitoring performance
	- Error detection
	- Surprise
- *Supervisory Attentional System*

 __Stroop Effect__: The automatic nature of word reading processes can interfere with other tasks relying on less-practiced/automated processes (such as a color-naming task)
 - In said task, making a mistake led to increased activity in the anterior cingulate cortex, and on the next trial
	 - The lateral prefrontal cortex activity increases on the trial after that as well only after previous ACC firing

__Error-Related Negativity__ (ERN): An ERP related to error-making
- Theoretically originating in the Anterior Cingulate Cortex

__Frontal Cortex__: Responsible for top-down inhibitory control
- In lesions studies, frontal lobe lesion patients presented with auditory clicks show greater activity than control patients
	- Implies that the frontal lobe functions as an inhibitor; filtering information based on what is attended to

__Rational Choice Theory__: People will always do what is in their best interest, maximizing their expected value of something
- Assumes that people are
	- Fully informed
	- Sensitive to subtle differences
	- Fully and always rational

__Expected Value__: An equation for describing the average reward of a probabilistic process. In other words, it is the sum of the probability/utility pairings for each possible outcome, denoted as
$$\text{Expected Value} = \text{Probability} \times \text{Utility}$$
- Utility can be calculated as positive or negative; it represents what you are getting/what they are taking away from you

__Dual System Model of Cognition__: Decision making relies on two separate process working in tandem to produce a specific decision
- *Hot*: Fast and automatic, processes items in parallel, reliant on context to inform its decision
	- Characterized by emotionally-driven decisions
	- Its circuitry is limbic:
		- Ventral Striatum
		- Orbitofrontal Cortex/Ventral Medial Prefrontal Cortex
- *Cool*: Slow, processes items serially, controlled and evidence-based
	- Characterized by rational decision processes
	- Its circuitry is part of "executive function"
		- Lateral Prefrontal Cortex
		- Dorsal Anterior Cingulate Cortex
		- Parietal Prefrontal Cortex

__Weber-Fechner Law__: As utility increases, our sensitivity to differences decreases
- When graphed, forms a logarithmic kind of slope

__Orbitofrontal Cortex__: Activates more as we value things higher
- Reflects value

__Dopamine__: Serves as a reward signal to how we value things
- Found in the Substantia Nigra and Ventral Tegmental Area

__Ventral Tegmental Area__: Plays a key role in reward evaluation
- Projects to Nucleus Accumbens, medial prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and the hippocampus

__Reward Prediction Error__: An equation for the difference between expected and received rewards, denoted as
$$
\delta(t) = r(t) + \gamma\hat{V}(t+1) - \hat{V}(t)
$$
- where;
	- $\delta`(t)$ is the reward prediction error (due to temporal difference in time $t$)
	- $r(t)$ is the actual reward given
	- $\gamma$ is the discount factor
	- $\hat{V}(t+1)$ is the reward predicted in the future
	- $\hat{V}(t)$ is the reward predicted in the moment
- In imaging studies, dopamine increases release when an unexpected reward occurs, stays the same when the reward matches expectations, and decreases when the reward is omitted
	- In other words, neurons do not encode rewards, but code changes in value and reward
- Dopamine in the vTA and SNc mimics the error function

__Temporal Discounting__: Getting the same reward has a greater subjective and real value
- Reflected in stronger and increased activation across the Ventral Striatum, Anterior Cingulate Cortex, and Orbitofronal Cortex
- This process is impaired when the orbitofrontal cortex is impaired
