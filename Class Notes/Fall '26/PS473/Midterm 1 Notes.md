### Science and research framework

- **McFall, Treat, & Simons (Clinical Science Model):** Scientific epistemology should be the centerpiece of clinical psychology, extending the Boulder model. Clinicians should think like scientists in every decision, with no “rigorous lab, intuitive clinic” split. The twin goals are advancing knowledge and improving public health.
- **Onken et al. (Reenvisioning Clinical Science):** There is a gap between efficacy research (internal validity) and effectiveness research (external validity). Effects shrink in the community, largely because of **treatment fidelity**. Development isn’t finished until an intervention is potent and implementable, which the non-linear **NIH Stage Model** captures.
- **Nielsen et al. (Science of Behavior Change):** Behavior change research should be mechanism-focused (the **experimental medicine approach**). Targets must be measurable, malleable, and causal. You identify a target, **engage** it with the intervention, and **validate** that changing it changes the outcome.

### Evidence and who gets treatment

- **Carpenter et al. (CBT meta-analysis of placebo-controlled RCTs):** CBT for anxiety disorders beats placebo. The size of the effect depends on the disorder and the type of placebo.
- **McHugh et al. (patient preference):** Patients prefer psychological treatment over medication by a wide margin.
- **Gunter & Whittal (dissemination):** Barriers to getting CBT to patients exist at the patient, therapist, and system levels, and each needs its own solutions.

### Diagnosis and assessment

- **Regier et al. (DSM-5 changes):** The DSM-5 reorganized chapters and moved toward dimensional and spectrum thinking, but it keeps a prototype-style categorical system.
- **Tolin et al. (DIAMOND):** A structured diagnostic interview shows good psychometric properties: reliability (e.g., inter-rater) and validity.
- **Lilienfeld, Wood, & Garb (projective techniques):** Broad dismissal is unwarranted, but most Rorschach, TAT, and drawing indexes lack empirical support. A few are valid, yet use in practice remains widespread.

### Cognition and transdiagnostic mechanisms

- **Bora (neurocognition in OCD relatives):** Unaffected relatives show deficits (inhibition, planning, decision-making, and smaller ones in set-shifting and visual memory), and patients are more impaired than relatives. This supports familial vulnerability markers (endophenotypes).
- **McEvoy & Mahoney (intolerance of uncertainty):** It mediates symptoms across several anxiety disorders and depression, so it is a transdiagnostic mechanism.
- **Nolen-Hoeksema et al. (Rethinking Rumination):** Rumination is a risk factor for depression and prolongs it. It separates into **brooding** (maladaptive) and **reflection**.
- **Fitzgerald et al. (anxiety sensitivity meta-analysis):** Anxiety sensitivity is malleable, and brief interventions can reduce it. This makes it a promising mechanistic target for prevention.

### Culture and delivery

- **Otto (stories and metaphors):** Metaphors make CBT concepts accessible and memorable (e.g., Limbic Kid, two TV screens).
- **Otto & Hinton (Cambodian refugees with PTSD):** Exposure-based CBT was modified for culture. It used cultural explanations like “wind”, group skill-teaching in a temple, interpreters, and interoceptive exposure.
- **McKay (career in neuropsychology):** This is a career-reflection piece, so it is lower yield for the exam.
