## Supporting in Crisis

__Crisis Intervention__: An immediate and short-term response to interrupt a psychological, emotional, physical, and behavioral effects of a stress-inducing event
- In crisis interventions, the main goal is to restore a person's ability to function

__Crisis Counseling__: An effective short-term intervention that aims to stabilize the emotional state of a person by
- Providing short-term support
- Keeping the person adn others around them safe
- Addressing their most critical needs
- Reducing the effects of the stress-inducing event
- Emphasize strengths and ability to cope

# III
>[!abstract]
>*In this module, you will learn about Crisis Text Line's commitment to support Texters in the least invasive way possible.*

>*Emergency service interventions are not the default—they’re the exception.*

__Least Invasive Interventions__: A collaboration over coercion to address risk of harm to self or others, empowering Texters to make a voluntary choice about safety based on their wishes, needs, and capacities by
- Attempting to de-escalate risk by building a connection and listening
- Developing a safety plan that includes a specific set of strategies and resources to manage current and future crises
- Assessing willingness and ability

__Involuntary Emergency Service Intervention__: A coercive way to address risk through involuntary involvement of law enforcement and hospitalization
- This form of intervention only happens in cases where the Texter is unwilling or unable to collaborate *and* they are at imminent risk of severe injury or death if said intervention is not initiated
		- When the Texter is unwilling to collaborate on safety, they:
			- Do not agree to reduce access to lethal means
			- Express intent to die by suicide
		- When the Texter is unable to collaborate on safety, they:
			- Have taken or are currently taking action to self harm to die by suicide *and* are physically unable to call 911 instead
			- Are experiencing life threatening symptoms *and* are unable to call 911
			- Are disconnected from reality *and/or* experiencing cognitive impairment due to substance abuse

__Imminent Risk__: Defined by one of four main types:
- Risk of suicide
	- Expressing a strong desire and intent to die by suicide while having the capability to follow through on their plan
	- Intending to follow through with their plan of suicide within 24 hours
	- Has already taken action or is in the process of taking action to end their life
- Risk of homicide
	- Expressing desire to kill one or more persons
	- Has a plan for how they would kill one or more persons
	- Has reasonable access to a method or tool to carry out the plan
	- Specified an intended target
- Risk of medical emergency
	- Discloses onset of an acute injury or illness with symptoms severe enough that a failure to receive medical care could reasonably result in loss of life
	- Has life threatening symptoms
	- Is physically unable to call 911 themselves or resonably believes that doing so will put their life in imminent danger
- Risk of a federal crime in progress
	- Discloses they are a victim or perpetrator of a crime, governed by federal law that is currently occurring or ongoing


>*Keep in mind that your Supervisor will only decide to do this after you both have exhausted all least invasive alternatives. If an emergency service intervention is needed, every effort will be made to obtain consent and foster as much autonomy as possible. Maintaining a collaborative approach can give Texters a sense of control, helps us advocate for the Texter’s expressed needs, and facilitates the process of locating the Texter and getting them the help they need.*

__Emergency Services Interventions__: Consist of two different types of interventions
- Public Safety Answering Point: A call center that receives and handles requests for dispatch of first responders
- Mobile Crisis Team: A group of trained professionals who respond to mental health or behavioral crises, often working alongside law enforcement. The following conditions must be met for their dispatch;
	- A mobile crisis team must be available in the Texter's area
	- The Texter *must* be at imminent risk for suicide or homicide and unwilling or unable to maintain their safety
	- The Texter must consent to dispatch of the mobile crisis team
	- If a Texter is at imminent risk for suicide or homicide and is unwilling or unable to maintain their safety, your supervisor will determine if mobile crisis teams is a possibility

>*Mobile Crisis teams are a preferred response whenever possible as they provide specialized and on-site support focused on mental health crises. In addition to their expertise in mental health, mobile crisis interventions minimize the use of traditional emergency services. However, if mobile crisis teams are unavailable or cannot be utilized, other emergency services must be utilized.*

>*Mobile crisis teams are not an appropriate intervention for Texters who are having a medical emergency, those who have taken or are currently taking action to end their life, or who are a victim or perpetrator of a federal crime in progress. Public safety answering points is a more suitable intervention for these types of imminent risk situations.*

>*You are not responsible for determining when an emergency service intervention is needed nor are you responsible for initiating it. However, you play an important role in the process. For every conversation you take, you will be responsible for notifying your Supervisor when a Texter is at imminent risk of suicide, homicide, medical emergency, or a federal crime in progress.*

>[!summary]
>- Least invasive interventions include:
>	- Attempting to de-escalate risk by building a connection and listening to the Texter's story
>	- Collaborating with the Texter to develop a safety plan that includes a specific set of strategies and resources to manage current and future crises
>	- Assessing the Texter's willingness and ability to secure their own safety or the safety of others by removing access to lethal means and/or to self-transport to a local urgent care facility
>	- Assessing whether there is a trusted person in close proximity to the texter who can keep a careful watch for possible danger or who can escort them to a local urgent care facility
>- Involvement of law enforcement can perpetuate stigma due to lack of training in mental health and it can result in escalation of violence due to the presence of weapons. Similarly, hospitalization can result in physical harm during psychiatric evaluations and cause financial strain due to transportation and treatment costs
>- Supervisors will only consider initiating an emergency service intervention in cases where the Texter is unwilling or unable to collaborate on safety and  they are at imminent risk of severe injury or death if an emergency service intervention is not initiated.
>- Texters at imminent risk of suicide, homicide, medical emergency, or federal crimes in progress might be eligible for a voluntary or involuntary emergency service intervention if all least invasive interventions have been exhausted.
>- Mobile Crisis teams are a preferred response whenever possible as they provide specialized and on-site support focused on mental health crises.
>- Volunteer Crisis Counselors are not responsible for determining when an emergency service intervention is needed or for initiating it. However, they are responsible for notifying their assigned Supervisor when a Texter is at imminent risk of suicide, homicide, medical emergency, or a federal crime in progress.

>*The overall goal of this course is to help you become more aware of the things that can impact your well-being and learn strategies to integrate wellness into your life. Throughout this module, you'll have the opportunity to reflect on what wellness means to you, gain awareness about the risks of volunteering in the mental health field, and identify strategies that support your wellness.*

__Wellness__: A state of physical, mental, and emotional wellbeing that is achieved by actively making choices that support a balanced and fulfilling life.
- Wellness is about listening to your body, mind, and intuition to figure out what makes you feel good

__Wellness Statement__: A written declaration of your vision for a fulfilling and healthy life
- It is less about what you want to do or accomplish, and more about what you want to feel.

__Countertransference__: How you think, feel, act, and relate to the Texter based on your own lived experience.

__Compassion Fatigue__: A state of emotional and physical exhaustion resulting from prolonged exposure to the emotional suffering of others.
- Compassion fatigue can result in difficulty connecting with others, as it is characterized by a reduced capacity for empathy, loss of interest in helping others, anger, and irritability

__Vicarious Trauma__: The emotional and psychological distress resulting from being exposed or hearing about trauma indirectly, or witnessing the traumatic experiences of others.

__Self-care__: The practice of taking an active role in protecting your well-being. It can take many forms, including:
- Social: Building and maintaining healthy connections with others
- Intellectual: Strengthening your knowledge and curiosity
- Spiritual: Connecting with your values and beliefs
- Emotional: Recognizing, channeling, and managing your emotions
- Environmental: Creating and maintaining spaces that make you feel safe and grounded
- Physical: Practices that nurture your wellbeing by caring for your body
- Financial: Reducing financial stress and uncertainty

>*Crisis Text Line strives to create a culture that is supportive and proactive about the well-being of Volunteer Crisis Counselors. Wellness is not just your responsibility, we are committed to help maximize your experience volunteering with us. Throughout your volunteering experience, you will have access to resources that will support your well-being on the platform and beyond.*

__Coaches__: Provide emotional support and suggest learning opportunities outside of the platform

>[!summary]
>- Crisis Text Line strives to support volunteers’ experience of well-being and belonging through holistic wellness programming and initiatives.
>- Wellness is holistic as it takes into account all aspects of a person’s life.
>- Constantly hearing about stories of trauma, grief, and other forms of suffering can result in countertransference, compassion fatigue, and vicarious trauma.

>[!abstract]
>*This module introduces three communication skills that will help you foster empathy, respect, and safety. To successfully embody these core competencies, you must be able to convey active listening, genuine concern, and warmth in every conversation.*

>*One of the most important characteristics of our services is that when Texters reach out for support, there is a real human on the other side of the screen that is ready to support them.*

__Active Listening__: The voluntary effort to understand the meaning and emotion behind what the other person is saying. this can be achieved by
- Minimizing distractions
- Avoiding assumptions
- Ask questions
- Fill in the blanks

__Genuine Concern__: Taking an active interest in the needs and feelings of others by acting with integrity and warmth. This can be achieved by:
- Mirroring the Texter's communication habits
- Keep the focus on the Texter
- Choose words with intention
- Don't over rely on example messages

>*Texters who report feeling like their Crisis Counselor is genuinely concerned for them are 10 times more likely to find the conversation helpful and 5 times more likely to report decreased thoughts of suicide by the end of the conversation*

>*Using a warm tone to support Texters undergoing emotional distress or a crisis will help you foster trust.*

A warm tone can be achieved by not:
- Asking "why" questions
- Sending standalone questions without additional context

>[!summary]
>- Human connection is essential in text-based crisis counseling—your presence, empathy, and responsiveness make a lasting impact.
>- Empathy means listening to understand, not just to respond. Use active listening techniques to pick up on both what is said and what is felt.
>- Empathy means listening to understand, not just to respond. Use active listening techniques to pick up on both what is said and what is felt.
>- Avoid assumptions—Texters are the experts on their own lives. Clarify, reflect, and check your understanding with respectful questions.
>- Respect is communicated through genuine concern—use honest, transparent, and Texter-centered language that affirms their lived experiences.
# VI
>[!abstract]
>*In this module, you will learn six good contact techniques that will help you respond to Texters effectively.*

__Good Contact Techniques__: Help build a strong connection with the Texter through
- Reflections: Rephrasing what's been heard to show active listening
- Strong Feeling Words: Describe and express emotions during a crisis
- Tentafiers: A way of interpreting how someone is feeling
  Validations: Recognize, normalize, and accept someone's thoughts, feelings, and experiences
- Strength Identifications: Describing someone's inherent positive qualities 
- Open-Ended Questions: Allow someone to expand on their thoughts and emotions

>*Good contact techniques amplify our ability to build trust and actively listen to texters when we don’t have face-to-face contact with them. We use good contact techniques throughout the conversation to let the texter know we believe what they’re telling us and their experiences are worth sharing. Most importantly, they help texters feel comfortable sharing what's really going on. Because they reflect and validate what the texter is experiencing, our use of these techniques in itself normalizes their feelings and strengthens our connection.*

| Technique                  | Why It's Important?                                                                                                                       | How To Use it?                                                                                                                           | When To Use It?                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| *Reflection*               | Shows that you are listening to the Texter and doing your best to put yourself in their shoes.                                            | Paraphrase what the Texter has said in your own words.                                                                                   | When you want to double-check that you’re understanding the Texter correctly, without having to ask.         |
| *Strong Feeling Words*     | Show the Texter you understand the intensity of their emotions and crisis.                                                                | Choose an emotion that matches the intensity of how the Texter is describing their experience.                                           | When a Texter is describing their situation without sharing how it’s impacting them.                         |
| *Tentafiers*               | Allows you to avoid making assumptions about what the Texter is feeling.                                                                  | Add a phrase at the beginning of your message to show the Texter that what you’re saying is just your interpretation of their situation. | To get the Texter to share more about a particular feeling without asking a question.                        |
| *Validations*              | Lets the Texter know that their feelings are normal and natural, and that there’s nothing wrong with them for feeling that way.           | Add it to your message to show the Texter it’s okay to feel whatever they’re feeling at the moment.                                      | When the Texter has just described a difficult emotion, or tries to minimize their own feelings.             |
| *Strength Identifications* | Shows the Texter that they have inherent positive qualities, and have the strength to fight through their struggles.                      | Look for the positive qualities in the actions a Texter has taken so far in dealing with their crisis and reflect those back to them.    | To build trust with the Texter and to remind them they do have some control over their feelings.             |
| *Open-Ended Questions*     | Allows the Texter to expand upon what they are feeling and thinking. Allows you to explore the situation without interviewing the Texter. | Ask Texter questions that help you understand what they’re dealing with, how they’re feeling, and how they’re coping with their crisis.  | To learn more and dig deeper into the Texter’s thoughts and feelings. To give added momentum when exploring. |
>[!summary]
>- Good contact techniques help volunteers demonstrate empathy, clarity, and support.
>- Use reflections to show understanding by paraphrasing.
>- Use strong feeling words to match the Texter’s emotional tone.
>- Use tentafiers to avoid assumptions and explore feelings gently.
>- Use validations to normalize feelings and create safety.
>- Use strength IDs to empower Texters by highlighting positive traits based on their actions.
>- Use open-ended questions to invite deeper sharing and avoid limiting responses.

>*This module will help you communicate with empathy and clarity to a range of Texters with varying backgrounds, identities, and experiences. This fosters inclusive and affirming interactions with Texters.*

__Depression__: A mood disorder that is characterized by persistent feelings of sadness, hopelessness, loss of interest in activities, and significant impairment in daily life. When supporting people with depression, it is important to
- Resist the urge to diagnose or give advice
- Be non-judgmental
- Use good contact techniques

__Loneliness__: The feeling of being disconnected from others. When supporting people experiencing loneliness, it is important to:
- Prioritize short-term support
- Pay attention to the intensity of their emotions

__Body Image__: The subjective perception of one's own body appearance

__Body Dissatisfaction__: A result in irregular eating behaviors that are often shaped by biological, psychological, social, and cultural factors. When supporting people with body dissatisfaction:
- Abstrain from reframing their beliefs
- Don't offer advice
- Respect the healing process
- Look out for life-threatening symptoms

__Image-Based Abuse__: Encompasses various forms of online violence/harassment that weaponize sexually explicit images of another person to control humiliate, or otherwise cause them harm. When supporting people experiencing such, it is important to:
- Be mindful of judgment
- Empower the Texter
- Highlight their strengths
- Be mindful of barriers to seek support

__Bullying__: A form of aggressive behavior that repeatedly causes harm to another person. When supporting people experiencing bullying, it is important to:
- Use validations
- Consider context and circumstances
- Differentiate from abuse

__Grief__: The emotional response a person experiences after a loss or perceived loss. When supporting someone experiencing grief, it is crucial to:
- Instill hope
- Not try to fix their pain

__Substance Use__: A broad term to describe the use of substances including alcohol and illicit drugs. When supporting someone experiencing substance use or abuse:
- Use the harm reduction model
- Practice being non-judgmental
- Look out for medical emergencies

When supporting people who identify as LGBTQ+, it is important to:
- Not make assumptions
- Use inclusive language
- Mirror their language
- Educate yourself
- Ask for help

__Interpersonal Conflict__: Disagreements or disputes that occur between two or more individuals. When supporting someone experiencing interpersonal conflict, it is important to:
- Keep the focus on them
- Follow their lead

__Sexual Violence__: A form of abuse, referring to any unwanted sexual act or attempt to obtain sexual act through violence, coercion. or force. When supporting someone experiencing sexual violence, it is important to:
- Validate their experience
- Emphasize that you believe them

__Domestic Violence__: Broadly defined as a pattern of behavior by one person in a relationship used to gain to maintain power and control over another

>[!summary]
>- People have complex, multifaceted identities and experiences, so always work to cultivate a safe space by offering warm, empathetic, and non-judgmental support.
>- Accepting the unique lived experiences of Texters is an important mindset to show respect.
>- Meet the Texter where they are, listen, and let them lead the conversation.

__Safety Assessment Model__: Divided into three phases
- Phase 1
	- Connect
		- *Build trust* by trying to understand the reasons they are seeking support and validating their experience to make them feel valued and understood
		- *Engage* by paying attention to any signs that they might be uncomfortable opening up and allow them to process their emotions at a pace they are comfortable with before...
	- Assess Immediate Safety
		- "*Have you had any thoughts of suicide in the last few days, including today?*"
		- "*Have you taken any action to harm yourself today?*"
		- *Intervene* if someone is at risk and informs you of any safety concerns
- Phase 2
	- Listen
		- *Seek to understand*
		- *Empathize*
	- Clarify
		- *You will clarify information with care to gain a complete understanding of the Texter’s situation.
		- *ask clarifying questions to understand the impact of the situation on their wellbeing*
- Phase 3
	-  Plan
		- *Support the Texter in identifying steps they will take after the conversation ends*
		- *Work with the Texter to design personalized ways of coping*
	- Wrap up
		- *The last step is to wrap*
