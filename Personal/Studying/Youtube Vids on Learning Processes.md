## [Study More Efficiently With These 2 Basic Steps](https://www.youtube.com/@JustinSung)

__Bloom's Taxonomy__: Divides learning into a hierarchy structure
	1. *Remember*: The most basic level; recall facts and concepts through definitions, listing, memorizing, repetition, etc.
	2. *Understand*: Explaining facts and concepts through classification, description, discussion, recognizing, reporting, translating, etc.
	3. *Apply*: Using learnt information in new contexts
	4. *Analyze*: Creating connections between distinct concepts through comparisons, differentiation, and general relating
	5. *Evaluate*: Justifying a concept or fact through judging value and critique
	6. *Create*: Produce new and original work built on learned concepts

__Structure of Observed Learned Outcomes__: Divides learning into a hierarchy of five steps, each assessing student's thinking quality
1. *Prestructural*: Unconnected and disorganized information
2. *Unistructural*: Simple connections without an importance hierarchy between information
3. *Multistructural*: More connections are made between multiple concepts; importance hierarchy still missing
4. *Relational*: Connections between concepts mean something beyond similarity, overall having more meaning to the person
5. *Extended Abstract*: Connections move beyond the scope in which they were originally taught, predicting and evaluating potential new connections to other concepts

__Linear Note Taking__: A traditional, sequential method of recording information based on standard outlines, bullet points, and numbered lists

__Principles of Proper Encoding__:
- Reach the highest levels of learning as quickly as possible
	- *To engage higher-order learning and create more efficient encoding, always try to relate information to each other and to the bigger picture*
	- The focus is to start applying higher-order processes as early as possible, where lower-level processes are acquired automatically
- Instead of writing notes straight away, practice a waiting period of "holding on to" information while asking
	- How does this relate to what I have learned?
	- How does this fit into the bigger picture/subject of what I am writing about?
## [How to Learn Anything Faster Using Modern Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TEDCHgZKXSY)

1. Practicing more leads to progress
	- Part of getting better isn't just about how many hours you rehearse something, but the way you do it matters
2. To become an expert, focus on just one thing
	- Hyperfixating on one domain (especially in spaces with flexible parameters such as the arts) leads to diminishing returns the more hours one practices. Instead, learning should be more generalized and dynamic in scope and techniques, such that one build conditional knowledge, or an understanding of what knowledge to apply based on different contexts
3.  To learn something properly, understand why rather than just memorize
	-  "Understanding" is also a form of shallow processing only slightly more deep than pure rote memorization, giving the illusion of deep understanding rather than what it actually is
4. To remember what you learned, write it down
	- Similar to "understand to learn properly", it is only a slightly more deeper processing than memorization, but shallow nonetheless. Unique to this piece of advice, however, is the idea that "writing = understanding"; rather, the value of writing notes lies in the processes that have to occur while writing notes (paraphrasing, key word analysis, summarizing, etc.). These processes are what lead to deep processing rather than the writing itself.
5. If something feels difficult to learn, fix the material and the way it is being taught
	- Learning is inherently difficult; it forces one to acquire new information and (when trying to make it stick) connect it to a larger map of concepts relating to said concept. Often a concept's difficulty is seen as a byproduct of the learning material's methodology, and while it is critical to learning, a concept's innate difficulty is also important to consider. In other words, a concept can simply be difficult to learn, no matter how much a teacher analogizes and contextualizes it. Therefore, it is important to acknowledge the quality of the concept's complexity as a critical factor in its learning, and instead also rely on oneself and the ability to conceptualize things through self work as ell.
6. Experience leads to better intuition
	- Experience is proportional to confidence rather than competence; the two are mixed up because of overconfidence biases. Since intuition is essentially high-level pattern-recognition, it is important to maximize learning experiences while implementing cognitive checks of confidence feedback. This build a more accurate intuitions and (ideally) a truer high competence

>*Improving learning is largely focused on applying better studying techniques; instead it is important to focus on the fundamental principles of learning.*

__Five Core Dimensions of Learning__:
- *Deep Processing*: The mental process that leads to better understanding and longer retention of information
	- It is important to understand concepts in the context of a bunch of other ideas in the same space/subject area
- *Self-Regulation*: Efforts towards changing and optimizing learning methods through active monitoring of mental feedback cues
	- The misconception of learning being inherently easy (if done effectively) leads to the Misinterpreted-Effort Hypothesis. In the context of this framework, misinterpretation occurs in the monitoring of cues; therefore it is critically important to not diagnose inefficiency due to learning methods being more difficult, since learning is inherently not easy.
- *Mindset*: The model by which one views learning and what to expect from the processes therein
- *Retrieval*: The ability of recalling information from memory
	- It is critical to practice the way you're going to be tested on the material
- *Self-Management*: The practices outside of the learning process that nevertheless affect learning
	- Since human behaviors do not exist in a vacuum, it is important to focus on adjacent and relevant actions in our lives that affect learning, especially things like time-management and distractions

## [7 Years of Building a Learning System in 12 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6GTt10GDWII)

__PERRIO__ (Priming, Encoding, Reference, Retrieval, and Interleaving Overlearning):
- *Priming*: Activities before a learning session that prepares the brain to process something before
- *Encoding*: Mental techniques that allow for more intuitive connections between concepts being learned, allowing for information to work in harmony rather than as separate ideas through:
	- Grouping
	- Analogies
	- Simplification
- *Reference*: The process of writing out raw information such that it can be visited, explored, and simplified later
- *Retrieval*: Self-testing to practice fluency and memory consolidation
- *Interleaving*: Testing in multiple ways using multiple techniques through multiple scopes to achieve a more complete understanding of a topic
- *Overlearning*: Repetitive learning practices with information beyond the required scope that results in a more broad and higher standard of understanding

## [Productivity is Hard Until You Build Systems Like This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AeV5F0ppaGw)

__Four Parts of a Productive System__:
- *Definition*: Understanding what the end goal of the system is; in other words, defining what "productive" even means
	- Productive is an end, rather than the means. In other words, productivity is defined by the end goals and therefore the machine built towards being productive should be built towards said goals, instead of being productive just to be productive due to moralization and dogma
- *Flow Matching*: Organizing one's own schedule based on task difficulty and energy levels through a day
- *Action Scripting*: Predetermining actions based on accounting for unknowns
	- For example, I will do X if A occurs
- *Action Architecture*: The groupings of cues and corresponding actions
	- Reliance of action based on motivation is not only inconsistent and unrealistic for things that need to get done, but also a fallacy since most complex higher-order behaviors are not habits. Additionally, habits are some of the hardest behaviors to form, and are themselves an amalgamation of complex behaviors. It is therefore crucial to break down said complex systems into more clear and actionable cues that lead to a behavior, such that structure emerges of cue-action pairings

>*Productive Systems are defined by their ability to set oneself up for productivity, such that there is a balance between day-of decision-making and pre-decided behaviors*

__When Thinking on Paper__:
- Focus on visualizing concepts as you would in your brain rather than a comprehensive and "correct" version of your notes by writing down keywords and later connecting said words
- Write shorter notes by not using full sentences and limiting aesthetic choices
- Reexamine and revise said notes by cleaning up connections and reorganizing the resulting mindmap
