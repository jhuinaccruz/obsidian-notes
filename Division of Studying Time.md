>*It is important to divide time such that there is a maximization of learning while minimizing the effort needed to effortfully plan such time. In other words, a basic structure of studying such that it is flexible enough to adapt depending on subject, time, and context while also being rigid enough to build habits around and for.*

>*Ultimately, a productive studying session (and more broadly system) is defined by how efficiently and strongly learning occurs and is retained.*

## Important Concepts

*Higher Level Processing* — Effective learning means that you reach the highest levels of learning as quickly as possible

*Division of Time* — It is not enough to just do one kind of studying; rather it is very helpful to dedicate specific blocks of time to specific behaviors (e.g. study questions, flashcards, quizzing, mindmapping, etc etc.)

*Less is more* — More writing/typing/longer notes are not more efficient, but rather it is important to maximize every word, drawing, and note's purpose

*Making Time*: Regardless of how busy life is, it is important to make time, because oftentimes it is necessary to study regardless of how life is going

*Planning*: Studying shouldn't just be something to decide to do; it is something to look ahead to and dedicate some time (earlier in the week, for example) previously

## Studying By Percentages

### The first 10%: Priming and Review

>*The primary purpose of this section is to "cast a wide net" for information that is relevant to what you're about to study*

Techniques can include:
- Skimming through previous notes
- Pre-lecture readings
- Reviewing previous reflections on what needs to be studied

### 70%: Studying

Since this is a very broad concept, there are important ways to break it down by
#### 25%: Encoding Reference Material

>*Essentially, this is where you read without taking notes yet. It is crucially important to read without immediately writing things down. In other words, this is where a lot of the cognitive load of learning occurs, when one is trying to create connections between concepts and what is being said such that it is easier to remember*

During encoding, it is really important to:
- Read (duh)
- Extract key concepts/words/ideas/abstracts
- Organize said ideas as they relate to one another

All of this process exists in the mind, which makes it abstract and hard to act upon, but the idea is to try to answer these questions in the process:
- Where does this fit?
- What does this explain?
- What is it similar to?
- What is different?
- Why is this important?

### 45%: Externalization

>*In some ways, externalization is essentially the creation of your own reference material. This process is different from simply reading somebody else's references, since the process of creating is critical to retention (and later retrieval). This is the part where writing notes and mind maps and whatever external source is being used occurs.*

### 10%: Self-Testing (Recall/Retrieval)

### 10% (Last 5 minutes): Reflection and/or Break
>*Perhaps the most important part; take a break, look at the broader picture of what you are studying and ask yourself what is going well, what is going not as well, and what more work needs to be done.*
