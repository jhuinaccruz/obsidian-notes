__Cochlea__: Vibrates with sound, allowing hair cells to transduce mechanical vibrations into neural activity

__Frequency__: Measured in Hz, where 1Hz is equal to one cycle per second

__Human range of hearing__: Approximately 20—20,000Hz

>*From the base of the cochlear duct to the apex, the range that is picked up decreases, such that the lowest frequencies are picked up at the apex*

__Weber-Fechner Principle__: The higher the quantity, the larger the just-noticeable difference is
- Ratios matter more than the difference

>*The Weber-Fechner Principle may be explained by how we perceive magnitude scales nonlinearly with the stimulus' magnitude

__Dichotic Listening__: An experiment where two distinct audio samples are played (one in each ear)
- Used to study attention and brain lateralization

__Attention__: Selecting certain information for further processing over other information

__Broadbent's Filter Model of Attention__: Also known as Broadbent's filter and the Bottleneck Model;
1. Messages enter into volatile sensory memory
2. Messages pass through a filter that cognitively selects what to attend to
	 - The message becomes the attended message, or the selectively-attended message
3. The attended message only is then semantically decoded and processed to then go to memory for retrieval and such

__Treisman's Attenuator Model__: Also known as Treisman's Attenuator and the Leaky Filter Method;
1. Messages pass through an "attenuator" that selectively lowers the volume depending on what is meant to be focused on
2. The attended and unattended message are both then analyzed for cues and semantic meaning
	- The attended message is more strongly chosen for analysis
3. The messages then go to memory for retrieval and such
