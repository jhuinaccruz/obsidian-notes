Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers that transmit electrical impulses (messages) beween neurons

Types of Neurons
- [[Serotonin]] (5-HT)
- [[Glutamate]] 
- Gamma aminobutyric acid ([[GABA]])
- [[Norepinepherine]] (NE)
- [[Dopamine]] (DA)
- [[Acetylcholine]]

## Designations:
-  __Classical__
	released due to an action potential
	acts at the synapse where it is released
	allows signals to be transmitted from neuron to neuron
		examples include [ Acetylcholine], [[Norepinepherine]], [[Dopamine]], [[Dopamine]], [[Serotonin]], [[GABA]], [[Glutamate]], [[Histamine]], [[Adenosine]], [[Cannabinoids]], [[Soluble Gases]]
-  __Neuromodulators__
	- alter the effectiveness of signal transmission
	- can be released at different times
	- examples include [[Substance P]], [[Oxytocin]], [[Opioid]]s

## Types of Neurotransmitter Receptors:
- ##### Pre-synaptic
	- __Ionotropic__ (directly affects the neuron)
		more direct, fast, short-lasting
	-  __Metabotropic__: indirectly affects (e.g. G-protein-coupled receptors)
		slower, longer-lasting, can affect multiple neurons
- ##### __Post-synaptic
	- __Excitatory Post-Synaptic Receptor__ (EPSP)
	- __Inhibitory Post-Synaptic Receptor__ (IPSP)

## Kinds of Receptor Interactions:
- __Agonist__
	A ligand that *initiates* or *enhances* a response
	- Partial agonist: high binding affinity, but only weakly activates the receptor
	- Full agonist: high binding affinity, and strongly activates the receptor
- __Antagonist__
	A ligand that binds to receptors and *blocks* the actions of a neurotransmitter
> Agonists and Antagonists can be __competitive__ (binds to the same (orthosteric) site as the original neurotransmitter) or __non-competitive__ (binds to a different (allosteric) site )
