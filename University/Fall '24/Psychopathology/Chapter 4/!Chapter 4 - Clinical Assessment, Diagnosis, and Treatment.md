Clinical Assessment: a systematic evaluation of psychological, biological, and social factors. The methods used to evaluate said factors are themselves evaluated through their
- [[Reliability]]
- [[Validity]]
- Standardization

__Diagnosis__: degree of fit between symptoms and diagnostic criteria
	In the United States, the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (5th Edition) or the DSM-5 is used

__Clinical interviews__ are _structured_ conversations used to understand important _context_ between the patient and the interviewer
>*People are often anxious before seeing a psychologist or other mental health professional for the first time. They may be particularly concerned about all of the questions they will be asked and wonder why some things, like perhaps family history or physical health, are relevant.*

__Mental health exams__ go deeper than clinical interviews, instead involving a number of psychological tests and evaluations, observing and measuring things such as
- Appearance and behavior
- Thought processes
- Mood and affect
- Intellectual functioning
- Sensorium

__Behavioral Assessments__ use direct observations to formally assess an individual's thoughts, feelings, and actions
>Target behaviors, antecedents. and consequences are identified
>May use analogue settings that are similar to real-life settings

## Diagnosing Psychological Disorders

- Classification: the categorization of people based on shared attributes or relations
- Taxonomy: *scientific* classification of organisms into a hierarchy
- Nosology: *medical* classification of diseases
- Nomenclature: the names and categories within a field of knowledge

## Approaches to Classification Systems

- Classical (Pure)
	- *Every* diagnosis has a *clear* cause and every disease is *unique*, therefore we only need *one* set of criteria for diagnosis 
	only particularly useful in medical applications (except within psychological contexts)
- Dimensional
	- Organized along a *series* of *continuums* rather than a *single* category. Said continuums include mood, cognition, and behaviors.
- Prototypical
	- Categorization is based on the *similarities* to other related objects/concepts
