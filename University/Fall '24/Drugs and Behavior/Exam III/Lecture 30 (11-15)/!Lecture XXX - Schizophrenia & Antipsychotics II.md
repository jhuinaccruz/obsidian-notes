## Theories
### Neurodevelopment/Hypofrontality Hypothesis
>There are defects in early brain development (particularly in frontal cortex), which results in reduced PFC activity
### Dopamine Hypothesis
>Schizophrenia is due to a *hyperactivation* of [[dopamine]] circuits (too much DA signaling)

Evidence lies in the fact that drugs that enhance DA activity can produce positive symptoms of schizophrenia (e.g. monoamine psychosis)
### Glutamate Hypothesis
>Schizophrenia is due to a hypoactivation of glutamate circuits (too little GLU signaling)

Evidence lies in that drugs can mimic some sympotoms of schizophrenia that are glutamate receptor antagonists (like PCP and Ketamine)

### Interactions Between Theories

>Early loss of cortical glutamatergic projection neurons lead to mesocorticolimbic system dysregulation

>In schizophrenia, neurodevelopmental issues cause reduced glutamate activity in the PFC - this leads to weakened mesocortical dopamine activation (negative/cognitive symptoms) and enhanced mesolimbic dopamine activation (positive symptoms)

## Antipsychotics

### History

__Pre 1950s__: no effective treatments for psychotic disorders like schizophrenia
	This resulted in turning to frontal lobotomies as a treatment, which (although it made them more docile), resulted in loss of executive function, personality and an inability to live independently
__1955__: Chlorpromazine was marketed in the United States as Thorazine with great success
	This drug led to the closure of many psychiatric hospitals in the US

### Kinds

#### Typical (First-Generation)

All developed before __1975__ and primarily D2 receptor antagonists (metabotropic)

This class of drugs is effective at treating the positive symptoms, but less effective at treating the other kinds of symptoms
	can also cause serious motor side effects

>A major side effect of first-generation "typical" antipsychotics is *neuroleptic* ("clasping the neuron") nigrostriatal motor system issues, known collectively as *extrapyramidal symptoms* or EPS reported in ~40% of patients

#### Atypical (Second-Generation)

More recently developed, they possess a weak affinity for D2 receptors, instead antagonizing D4 receptors and 5HT-2A receptors, for which they have a higher affinity for

>Serotonin modulation (particularly in PFC) is thought to improve mood and reduce risk of EPS

>...only about one third of individuals respond well to the drug, and they often come with unwanted side effects of weight gain and sedation

#### Third-Generation

>in __2003__, the FDA approved a new drug, aripiprazole for the treatment of schizophrenia

Unlike previous generations, it is a competitive DA receptor partial agonist, and modulated DA activity at D2, D3, and D4 receptor subtypes-known as a "dopamine system stabilizer"

It has a high affinity for Dopamine receptors, but it has a reduced efficacy

In the mesolimbic system, it weakly binds to the DA receptors and almost acts like an antagonist, reducing positive symptoms

In the mesocortical system, it enhanced DA signaling and reduces negative and cognitive symptoms

Side effects (less strong than its predecessors) includes motor restlessness (akathisia), minor weight gain, insomnia, nausea, and GI issues

### Pharmacokinetics
#### Administration
Usually given orally, or in a slowly dissolving depot injection
	Not accelerated by IV injection

#### Absorption/Distribution
Anti-psychotics are readily absorbed from the digestive tract

Easily crosses [[Blood Brain Barrier]]

Absorbed into body fat and released slowly

#### Metabolism/Elimination
Typicals/Atypicals are metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes and have half lives of (11-58 hours)

## Effects

>Also trouble with temperature regulation (easily influenced by changes in the environment), weight gain, changes in cardiac function, and blood pressure

#### Subjective Effects

- Tiredness
- Slowed and confused thinking
- Trouble concentrating
- Anxiety and irritability
- Internal restlessness
- Patient uncompliance

#### Performance Effects
- sedating effects
- lowering of sex drive
- inconclusive on cognitive effects overall

### Tolerance, Withdrawal, and Lethality

>Essentially, tolerance doesn't develop

>Physical dependance is rare or very mild

>Extremely safe with a very high therapeutic index (~100-1000!)
