## Study Questions
### Chapter 5: Substance Use and Addictive Disorders 
1. Describe the disease model of addiction. What are some criticisms of this model? Describe the dependence model of addiction. What are some criticisms of this model? Describe the positive reinforcement model of addiction. What are some criticisms of this model? 
	
	*The disease model believes that physiological changes (caused by drugs) lead to addiction. Critics of this view challenge that this ignores the social factors of individual's life. The dependence model of addiction believes that withdrawal is so painful and distressing that people are willing to avert the consequences at all cost. However, critics point to the fact that some highly addivtive drugs produce less severe withdrawal, as well as that some non-addictive drugs (like topical steroids) produce withdrawal effects and show no evidence of being addictive. The positive reinforcement model suggests that the addiction comes from the desirable effects of the drug. However, it is challenged due to drug tolerance.*

2. Describe the hedonia hypothesis of dopamine function. What evidence exists to contradict this hypothesis? Describe the central concepts of the incentive sensitization theory of drug addiction.

	*The Dopamine/Hedonia Hypothesis was coined by Olds and Milner in the 1950s, and it suggests that the mesolimbic pathway is the "pleasure-center" of the brain. However, it has been challenged on multiple levels, such as the fact that Parkinson's disease (a disease caused by dopamine deficiencies) can still experience pleasure. The Incentive-Sensitization theory suggests that dopamine increases the salience of drug and drug-related consumption.*

3. Compare and contrast the effects of drug reinforcers and natural reinforcers on the brain and behavior. In what respects are they similar and how do they differ?

	*Drug reinforcers are often unstable and result in a cycle of continued use due to their bypass of satiation mechanisms. Natural reinforcers have satiation mechanisms that allow controlled consumption. *

4. Describe the central concepts of the hedonic dysregulation theory of drug addiction. Provide research-based examples of how deficits in information and higher-order processing impact drug addiction.

	*Hedonic disregulation says that addiction comes from three stages: bingeing, withdrawal, and anticipation.*
