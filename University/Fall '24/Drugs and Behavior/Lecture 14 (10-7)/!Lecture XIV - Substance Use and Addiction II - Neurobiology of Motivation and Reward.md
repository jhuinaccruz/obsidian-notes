>[!abstract] Learning Objectives:
>- Learn about brain systems controlling motivation, reward and reinforcement: the mesolimbic, mesocortical, and nigrostriatal pathways
>- Understand which components of the “motivation control system” of the brain underlie behavioral “activation” vs. “guidance” toward a reinforcing stimulus
>- Recognize the importance of early reinforcement studies on the proposed role of dopamine in reward, the key differences between naturally rewarding stimuli (e.g. palatable food) vs. addictive drugs (e.g. heroin), and the importance of route of administration in the addictive potential of a drug (if time permits)

>The [[Mesolimbic Dopamine System]] (VTA —> NAc) plays important roles in reinforcement, motivation, and reward seeking, while the [[Mesocortical Pathway]] is important for planning & learning

>Dopamine neurons in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) also send axons to the basal ganglia (BG) along with projections from the substantia nigra (SN), which form a motor loop to activate motor behavior

Drug addiction affects these dopamine pathways which results in lasting neural adaptations

## Activation

- the Mesolimbic Pathway (__Ventral Tegmental Area__ to __Nucleus Accumbens__)
	more largely, the __Ventral Striatum__ includes the VTA, nAc, as well as the __ventral pallidum__ and the __olfactory tubercule__
	- all the structures in the striatum play important roles in motivation *and* reinforcement

- the Nigrostriatal Pathway (from the Substantia Nigra to the Basal Ganglia)
	responsible for the motor movements of the body
	- the structures form a "motor loop"

## Guidance

- the Mesocortical Pathway (VTA to Prefrontal Cortex)
	pathway from VTA to the cortex, amygdala, septum and the hippocampus
	- nicknamed the "learning and memory circuitry"
	- helps *guide* the animal to reinforcers, short term memory, planning, etc.

## Dopaminergic Synapse

>There are approximately one million “dopaminergic neurons” in the human brain whose cell bodies are mostly found in the VTA and SN

Dopaminergic neurons synthesize and release dopamine

>In the mesocorticolimbic system, dopamine mainly binds to metabotropic D1 and D2 dopamine receptors

The dopamine transporter (DAT) expressed on the presynaptic neuron

## Studies

### Olds and Milner (1950s)
They discovered that certain areas in the brain when electrically stimulated would help reinforcement
- They concluded there is a *discrete "pleasure center"* in the brain

>Dopamine is released in the mesolimbic system in response to natural and artificial “rewards”

## Natural versus Artificial Rewards
### Natural
- have __satiation mechanisms__ that turn off the behavior after a certain point
### Artificial
- drugs can bypass satiation mechanisms, resulting in a cycle of continued use
- drugs can also have a greater intensity and immediacy (bioavailability) of their effects
	- such intensity and immediacy makes reinforcement of their consumption that much stronger
