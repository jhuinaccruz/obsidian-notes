## 1. Causative-Passive Sentences
>*“Causative-passive” sentences are the passive version of causative sentences. You can use causative-passive sentences when you want to say that you were made to do, or harassed or talked into doing, something that you did not want to*

Causative-passive sentences follow the following form:
__Puppet/Subject__ は __Puppet Master__ に action (verb in passive-causative form)

| verb             | causative-passive      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- |
| る・す-ending verbs | remove る・す, add 〜させられる |
| う-ending verbs   | remove う, add 〜あされる    |
| する (exception)   | させられる                  |
| くる (exception)   | こさせられる                 |
## 2. ~ても
>*“A ても B” is “B, even if A.” That is, B is still true in case of A (so is certainly true if A is not the case).*

|                    | 〜ても form            |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| verbs              | (te form) + も       |
| い-adjectives       | (te form) + も       |
| な-adjectives/nouns | adjective/noun + でも |

## 3. 〜ことにする
>*〜ことにする means “decide to do . . ." It follows the short form present tense of a verb. You can use a negated verb, too.*

>*We sometimes use the volitional form of this construction, such as 行くことにしよう instead of the simple volitional form of a verb, 行こう in suggesting an activity. 行くにしよう has the additional implication that the suggestion is being made after a deliberation.*

## 4. 〜まで
>*A まで  means “till A.” The A in “A まで B” is the description of the change that coincides with or causes the end of B. The A, therefore, is usually a verb of the “change” kind (see Lesson 7).*

## 5. 〜方
>*The noun-forming suffix follows the stem of a verb and means “the way in which the action is performed” or “how to do ....”*

>[!important]
>*Nouns that are marked with other particles when they go with a verb are marked with のbefore 〜方*

With compound する verbs, mark the noun with の
