## Overview
>[!abstract]
>*Sexual and gender minority (SGM) individuals are at increased risk for experiencing sexual violence. Bystander intervention training programs are a first-line prevention recommendation for reducing sexual and dating violence on college campuses. Little is known regarding the extent to which SGM individuals are represented in the content of bystander intervention programs or are included in studies examining the effectiveness of bystander intervention programs. The present critical review aimed to fill this gap in knowledge. Twenty-eight empirical peer-reviewed evaluations of bystander intervention programs aimed at reducing dating violence or sexual assault on college campuses were examined. Three studies (10.7%) described including content representing SGM individuals in the program. Personal communication with study authors indicated that—although not mentioned in the publication—many programs describe rates of violence among SGM students. When describing the study sample, six studies (21.4%) indicated that transgender, nonbinary, or students classified as “other” were included in the research. Approximately two thirds of studies (67.9%) did not describe participants’ sexual orientation. No studies reported outcomes specifically among SGM individuals, and two (7.1%) mentioned a lack of SGM inclusion as a study limitation. Work is needed to better represent SGM individuals in the content of bystander intervention programs and ensure adequate representation of SGM individuals in studies examining the effectiveness of bystander intervention programs.*

__Research Question__: How is current research accounting for sexual minorities in bystander intervention research, both as participants, victims, and as bystanders?

## Findings

__Contents of Programs__:
- 3 (10.7%) represented represented SGMs in their contents:
	- As (Male) victims of sexual violence
	- As a separate category of relationships (same-sex)
	- As a non-heterosexual category of relationships (e.g. differing gender/sexual identities)
- 6 (21.4%) studies denoted a category of non-female sexual victims
- 1 study reported transgender participants chose to identify between male/female populations

__Participant Characteristics__:
- No study included intersex as a categorization
- Most studies did not report sexual orientation of participants
- 2 studies reported gay participants
- 1 study reported lesbian participants
- 4 studies reported bisexual participants
- 1 study reported non-exclusively heterosexual identities

__Results__:
- No study reported direct outcomes between SGM identities
- No study differentiated outcomes between SGM and majority identities
- 2 studies reported transgender population exclusion in analyses due to low sample sizes
- 1 study reported transgender population inclusion in analyses
- One study noted increased heterosexual participation in follow-up surveys

__Limitation Sections__:
- 15 (53.6%) studies did not mention a SGM inclusion
- 11 (39.3%) studies mentioned a broad lack of diversity

__Other__:
- Studies portraying men sexual victimization did so with the intention of evoking empathy to females' own experiences
## Interpretations
- Lack of reported inclusion of sexual minorities within bystander intervention literature
	- Leads to a current gap in understanding of how current bystander account for SGM needs are met
- Lack of reported inclusion may perpetuate conceptions of sexual violence being a women-only issue
- It is important to account for allow space for a nuanced description of one's own identity
- Studies rarely mention limitations regarding diversity and inclusion of SGM
- Despite lack of SGM in relation to the general public, more work is necessary to include them in analyses
## Limitations
- Analyses were only of bystander programs (multiple other forms of prevention programs exist)
- No systematic review
- Relied only on provided information
